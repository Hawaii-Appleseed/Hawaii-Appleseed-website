2027 legislative session

Our 2027 Priorities

Local families are leaving because they can’t afford to stay, while our tax code rewards those who profit most from our islands. In 2027, the Hawaiʻi Tax Fairness Coalition is working on three changes that ask the wealthiest to pay their fair share and keep money in the pockets of working families.

Raise revenue fairly

  1. 1

    Increase the Tax on Luxury Homes

    Modernize the tax on property sales and dedicate its revenue to the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands.

    Up to $60M a year

    for Hawaiian Home Lands under last session’s House proposal

    Read more about the luxury homes tax →

    The conveyance tax is paid when real estate changes hands. Its rates haven’t changed since 2009, and none of its revenue goes to the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands (DHHL).

    29,589applicants on DHHL’s homestead waitlist (Nov. 2025)
    $303Mtotal conveyance tax revenue by FY2031 under the House proposal
    • Ask more of high-value and investment property sales, while protecting local families buying a home to live in.
    • Dedicate the new revenue to DHHL, so the trust can build homes for its beneficiaries.

    Estimate: House committee report, SB 3028 SD2 HD1 with the rates of HB 2049 HD3 (2026), FY2027–FY2031; applies to residential property.

  2. 2

    Tax Profits Like Paychecks

    Tax capital gains at the same rates as ordinary income.

    Over $100M

    estimated new revenue each year

    Read more about taxing profits like paychecks →

    Wages are taxed at up to 13 percent. Profits from selling stocks, land, and other investments are capped at 7.25 percent.

    90%of the new revenue would come from the top 1 percent
    5.75 ptsgap between the top rate on wages and on capital gains
    • Taxing capital gains like wages would raise over $100 million a year.
    • Most local families would pay nothing more.

    Estimate: Hawaiʻi Appleseed tax model, tax year 2027, Hawaiʻi residents only, accounting for fewer gains being realized at a higher rate.

Protect working families

  1. 3

    Preserve Working Families’ Tax Credits

    Extend the earned income and food/excise tax credits before they expire, and protect the child and dependent care tax credit.

    $76M*

    that working families would lose in 2028 if the credits expire

    *Earned Income Tax Credit and refundable food/excise tax credit only.

    Read more about working families’ tax credits →

    Act 163 (2023) doubled the state Earned Income Tax Credit and the refundable food/excise tax credit. Those expansions end after the 2027 tax year unless lawmakers act.

    78,000Hawaiʻi households claim the state EITC each year
    $156Mclaimed through the EITC, food/excise, and child and dependent care credits in 2023
    • The child and dependent care tax credit also helps working parents with the cost of childcare.

    Estimates: Hawaiʻi Appleseed tax model, tax year 2028; EITC claims from the Department of Taxation, tax year 2024; credit totals from the Department of Taxation’s Tax Credits Claimed report, tax year 2023.
