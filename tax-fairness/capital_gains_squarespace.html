Tax Profits Like Paychecks

Tax capital gains at the same rates as ordinary income.

Hawaiʻi taxes a nurse’s paycheck at higher rates than an investor’s profit from selling stock or land. We want to close that gap.

The loophole

A nurse or a hotel worker pays Hawaiʻi’s regular income tax rates on every paycheck. But profits from selling stocks, land, and other investments are capped at 7.25 percent, even for the highest-income households, whose wages are now taxed at up to 13 percent.

  • Top rate on wages13%
  • Capital gains rate7.25%

Act 24 (2026) raised the top rate on wages but left the capital gains rate alone, so the gap between work and wealth grew from 3.75 to 5.75 percentage points.

What we are asking for

Tax capital gains at the same rates as ordinary income, so income from owning is taxed like income from working.

What it would do

  • Raise about $179 million in 2027 ($130 million from residents and $49 million from nonresidents).
  • Collect about 92 percent of the resident revenue from the top 1 percent of households. Most local families would pay nothing more.
  • A 9% cap on capital gains would raise about $67 million ($48 million from residents and $19 million from nonresidents).

Estimate: Hawaiʻi Appleseed estimate, Census-Forecaster tax model, tax year 2027, Act 24 brackets, accounting for fewer gains being realized at a higher rate.
