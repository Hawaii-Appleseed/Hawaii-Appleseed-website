Method + budget-link mockups

Everything below is live: change the threshold or rate in any revenue box and all of them update together. Part 1 is how the tax connects to the budget blocks. Part 2 is how the page explains where $1.41B comes from.

Part 1 — Tie the revenue to the budget blocks

Today the 12.4 blocks above and the 6.0% meter below say the same thing in two different pictures. These make them one picture: one block is a full year of the state budget, and the tax fills a slice of it.

B1 — A mint slice inside the first block

The first block of the big bar gets a bright slice that grows and shrinks as you change the rate. A caption under it says what it is. The meter below stays as it is.

B2 — Hundred squares

The meter becomes a 10×10 grid: the whole grid is one year’s budget, lit squares are what the tax raises. Self-contained; doesn’t touch the dark card.

B3 — The meter becomes a block

Same idea as B1 but at the bottom: the thin meter is redrawn as one of the sage blocks from the bar above, with the mint slice in it, and the caption says “same block as above.”

Part 2 — Show where the number comes from

All three keep the current layout of the box. Each one shows the working for whichever threshold and rate is selected.

A1 — “How we estimated this” expander

A pill under the box opens a short worked calculation, what the estimate leaves out, and the source. Closed by default on the real page; shown open here (click it).

A2 — The equation is always on screen

Replaces the one-line footnote with the arithmetic as a row of chips: held, minus the untaxed first $20M each, equals taxable, times the rate, equals the revenue. Assumptions fold out underneath.

A3 — ⓘ at the number, full “Method and sources” section at the bottom

A small ⓘ next to “Could raise each year” opens a one-line formula (shown open here). The full explanation lives in a light card further down the page, in the same style as “Why it matters.”

Worth confirming before I build: the budget line says “$23.7B, the state’s annual budget (2026)” exactly as the page does today. If that figure is the general-fund budget or the all-funds budget, the method text should say which. I haven’t guessed.
