“Tax wealth above” — mockups

All five boxes are live: change a control in any one and the others follow. Only the controls area changes; the number, the budget share and the “How we estimated this” button are the same. In S1–S3 I also moved “applied only to the $70.7B…” up into the caption under the number, so each fact is said once instead of three times.

Now (for comparison)

The controls sit in a cell that looks just like the two result cells, so they read as a third result. “Tax wealth above” doesn’t say it’s per person. And “2% on wealth above $20M” is then repeated under the number and again in the footnote.

S1 — One sentence

The two controls become the first line of a sentence, “If Hawaiʻi taxed each person’s wealth above $20M at a rate of 2%…”, and the results below finish it. The inputs get their own filled panel, so they stop looking like a third result card. This is the biggest change in how it reads.

S2 — Scenario grid

All six scenarios from the report at once. Rows are the threshold (with how many residents it covers), columns are the rate, and each cell is the answer. Tap a cell and the big number and budget share follow. Nothing to decode, and the whole range is visible without touching anything. It’s the tallest option.

S3 — Who’s taxed

Keeps today’s three-cell strip, but the threshold choice explains itself: $20M covers 1,245 residents, $50M covers 365. Both controls now run the full width of the cell so they line up. The smallest change; it answers “above what, and who does that hit?”

S4 — Number first

Flips the order: the answer is the headline, and the “how” is one line under it with the two pills inline. Only two cells, so the box is shorter and the number gets more room. The footnote stays as it is today.
