Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HB1473 – Relating to Housing
House Committee on Housing
Tuesday, March 19, 2025 at 1:00PM Conf. Rm.  and via Videoconference
________________

Aloha Chair Evslin, Vice Chair Miyake, and members of the committee;
Mahalo for the opportunity to testify in strong support of HB1473, which would establish tenant opportunity to purchase provisions to help preserve Hawaii's critically needed affordable housing stock. We also offer comments to the proposed conveyance tax structures that are intended to incentivize these opportunities. [a]
This legislation addresses one of Hawaii's most pressing challenges - the potential loss of thousands of affordable housing units as their affordability restrictions expire. Additionally, it addresses the lack of legal mechanisms for tenants and nonprofits to intervene when these properties are sold.
As noted in the bill, the HHFDC alone has 2,000 units in its low-income housing tax credit program scheduled to expire in xx years. When taking into consideration the other subsidized housing in the state, AARP estimates the potential loss of **** affordable housing units by ***.  This is particularly concerning at a time when Hawaii already faces a severe affordable housing shortage. 
When affordable properties convert to market rate, it often leads to displacement of long-term residents who cannot afford dramatic rent increases. This can largely affect households negatively emotionally, physically, and can contribute to increased rates of homelessness.
By requiring landlords to grant a 45-day exclusive period for tenants and other eligible parties (such as nonprofits, local governments, and community land trusts) the first opportunity to purchase, and an additional 60 day period to match any non eligible entity offer, HB1473 gives residents and community organizations a chance to preserve their homes or commit to affordability. 
The tenant opportunity to purchase approach has demonstrated success in other jurisdictions. For example, Washington D.C.'s Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act (TOPA) has helped preserve over 16,000 affordable housing units over the history of the (https://thecoalitiondc.org/news/cnhed-releases-comprehensive-analysis-of-dcs-tenant-opportunity-to-purchase-act-topa/#:~:text=The%20act%20has%20significantly%20contributed,sale%20of%20their%20rental%20homes / https://thecoalitiondc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/CNHED_TOPAStudyNov09.pdf ). Through TOPA, many affordable units have been preserved that would otherwise have converted to market rate.[b]
