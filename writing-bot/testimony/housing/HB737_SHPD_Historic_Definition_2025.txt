Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HB737 – Historic Preservation; Historic Property
House Committee on Water & Land
Tuesday, February 11, 2025 at 10:00AM Conf. Rm. 411  and via Videoconference
________________

Aloha Chair Hashem, Vice Chair Lamosao, and members of the committee;
Mahalo for the opportunity to testify in strong support of HB737 HD1, which would update Hawaii's  "historic property" definition to better reflect properties that merit preservation based on their cultural, historical, and architectural significance, rather than age of structure.
HB737 HD1 streamlines the historic review process by redefining "historic property" to properties that meet the criteria outlined in either the Hawaii or National Register of Historic Places, or properties that are located in a designated historic district, and adds Native Hawaiian cultural importance as an eligible criteria for historic definition. The bill establishes that historic review for projects on privately-owned single-family homes or townhouses will only be required under this new historic definition, allowing the State Historic Preservation Division (SHPD) to focus resources more effectively, ultimately reducing review delays and associated costs.
The current law's 50-year age threshold creates an inefficient system that delays development while failing to effectively protect truly significant historical sites, as it prioritizes age over historic and cultural importance. This blanket requirement adds to Hawaii's already strictest-in-nation regulatory barriers, which drives construction costs to triple the national average, and burdens the department with reviews of routine maintenance.[1] These unnecessary delays and costs for older properties contribute directly to Hawaii's housing affordability crisis.
This issue is particularly critical to address as the 50-year threshold affects a significant percentage of Hawaii's housing stock. According to national data, no state in the US has a housing stock that is aging more quickly than Hawaiʻi. As of 2021, the median aged home was built in 1980, with an approximate age of 41 years old.[2]  Without intervention, we can anticipate a substantial increase in SHIPD’s workload, leading to longer delays and higher associated costs.
We strongly urge the committee to pass HB737 HD1, which represents an opportunity to preserve Hawaii's genuine historical and cultural heritage while reducing unnecessary regulatory burdens that exacerbate our already high cost of housing.
Mahalo for the opportunity to testify.
