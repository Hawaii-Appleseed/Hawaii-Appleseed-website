Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for S.B. 961 – Relating to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program
House Committee on Human Services and Homelessness
Thursday, March 13, 2025, at 10:00AM
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Marten, Vice Chair Olds, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of S.B. 961, which would require the Department of Human Services (DHS) to adjust the minimum certification periods for Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits households, participate in the Elderly Simplified Application Project (ESAP), and report to the Legislature.

SNAP is a critical lifeline for more than 160,000 Hawaiʻi residents, including approximately 31,000 seniors over the age of 60. Without it, many of these individuals would not have any money to pay for food at all.  Research has shown that older adults who are enrolled in SNAP actually see improvements in health outcomes, including:
* Less costly hospital stays and fewer hospital admissions,[1]
* A decrease in skipping medication doses to save money and/or delay refills,[2]
* Fewer nursing home admissions.[3] 
Being enrolled in SNAP eases the burden of food costs for our kūpuna, who already have to balance medical expenses and Hawaiʻi’s high cost of living. With ESAP, our state has the opportunity to streamline the process, as many of those who qualify struggle to maintain benefits because of the complicated recertification process.

Since benefits are federally funded, federal rules require tedious paperwork and documentation both upon initial application and for periodic recertification.[4] Some SNAP users lose these crucial benefits during the recertification process, despite no changes to eligibility. This phenomenon, known as “churn,” adds significantly to the administrative burden of administering SNAP at the state level.

This bill would provide some relief for the churn issue. Federal rules allow states to extend the SNAP certification period to 12 months for most households, with just a six-month “interim report.” However, Hawaiʻi currently requires a full recertification every six months, increasing the possibility for churn. For households with seniors or people with disabilities, Hawaiʻi allows a longer 12-month certification, but for these households, it can be as long as 24 months, or even up to 36 months using what’s known as the ESAP waiver. In addition to the three-year certification period, this waiver allows those who are 60+ to waive the recertification interview and allows for a simplified application process. As of 2024, 23 other states have already implemented ESAP and there are more to follow suit.[5] 

Table 1. Hawaiʻi certification periods vs. maximum federal certification periods
Group
	Hawaiʻi’s current certification period
	Maximum allowable certification period
	Kūpuna (ages 60+) or adults with disabilities
	Every 12 months
	Every 36 months*
	All other households
	Every 6 months
	Every 12 months
	*36 months is only allowable as part of the ESAP waiver; 24 months requires a simpler waiver application

The proposed extended certification periods for most households have twofold benefits, reducing workload for both applicants and eligibility staff. We have a unique opportunity to ease the burden on both sides of the application process. Thank you for considering these comments in your deliberation of S.B. 961.
