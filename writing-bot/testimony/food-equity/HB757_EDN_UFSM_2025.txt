Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HB 757 – Relating to Education
House Committee on Education
Tuesday, February 11, 2025, at 2:01pm
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Woodson, Vice Chair La Chica, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of HB 757, which would appropriate funds to the Department of Education (HIDOE) to provide up to two free, nutritious meals to all students, regardless of their financial situation.

School meals are one of the government’s most powerful tools for delivering good nutrition to students. In Hawaiʻi, there are still three in 10 households with children facing food insecurity.[1] This means that for many children in Hawaiʻi, school breakfast and lunch are the most nutritious meals they eat in a day. 

Despite this, many students in Hawaiʻi are not getting the nutrition they need. In some cases, their families don’t meet the strict eligibility thresholds for free or reduced-price meals, yet still struggle to stretch their budgets. For example, a family of four that makes $47,000 per year doesn’t qualify for free lunch,[2] while Hawaiʻi’s costs of living require that same family to make over $107,000 to simply survive.[3] In other cases, even students who do meet the eligibility requirements can slip through the cracks. In fact, one study found that 34 percent of students not receiving school meals were actually eligible to receive them.[4] 

Hawaiʻi still allows schools to deny meals to students who have a negative meal balance, leaving students not only hungry but also ashamed. One student testifying asking the legislature to pass this bill last year stated, “Earlier this year I ran out of money on my lunch account and the lady sent me to put my food back because I didn't have enough…I wouldn't want another student to feel that type of embarrassment.” Implementing UFSM would serve as a vital safety net, ensuring no child goes hungry during school hours and fostering a more equitable educational environment.
________________

Research consistently links proper nutrition to better healthy development and learning outcomes.[5] Universal free school meals would ensure that every student in Hawaiʻi has access to the nutrients necessary for optimal learning, helping to close achievement gaps. One public school teacher put it this way:
 “We put a great deal of emphasis on student outcomes such as test scores. However, if we want to improve student output we must address student input such as access to qualified teachers and healthy meals… Providing students with universal free lunch may also be an important tool to help decrease the chronic absenteeism that has plagued schools ever since the Covid-19 Pandemic.”
 — Hawaiʻi public school teacher
Finally, UFSM would ultimately support Hawaiʻi’s local farmers, through the DOE’s other school meal initiatives, notably Farm-to-School. This not only provides students with healthier, more sustainable food options but also strengthens Hawaiʻi’s economy. Reducing the state’s reliance on imported food fosters a more resilient and self-sufficient community.
Thank you for the opportunity to provide testimony in support of HB 757. As shown in the attached page, there are multiple revenue options that would cost the DOE only 1% of its annual budget. Across the country, UFSM has been implemented successfully through the intentional blending of policies and funding mechanisms at the federal, state, and local levels. We are pleased that the Hawaiʻi legislature is paying attention to this important issue because hungry keiki cannot learn. Thank you for your consideration of this bill.
  

________________

The Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice advocates for economic justice for and with Hawaiʻi’s people. We envision a Hawaiʻi that puts its people first—where everyone can meet their basic needs while living happy, healthy and creative lives.
