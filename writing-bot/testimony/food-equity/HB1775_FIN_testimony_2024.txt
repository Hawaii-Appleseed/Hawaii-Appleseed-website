Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for H.B. 1775 – Relating to Education
House Committee on Finance 
Friday, February 23, 2024, at 1:00PM
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Yamashita, Vice Chair La Kitagawa, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of H.B. 1775, which would appropriate funds to the Department of Education to provide a free, nutritious breakfast and lunch to all public school students, regardless of their ability to pay. 

School meals are one of the government’s most powerful tools for delivering keiki good nutrition 
Adequate nutrition is essential for children’s health, learning, and long-term development. However, there are still up to one in four children in Hawaiʻi that regularly go without enough nutritious meals at home.[1] This means that for many children in Hawaiʻi, school breakfast and lunch are the most nutritious meals they eat in a day. 

Universal free school meals are a lifeline to Hawaiʻi’s struggling families
Federal eligibility for free or reduced price meals requires that households earn below 130% or 185% of the federal poverty level ($44,850 or $63,825, respectively, annually for a family of four in 2024). However, Hawaiʻi’s costs of living require that a family with two adults and two children make at least $104,000 to simply survive.[2] This leaves Hawaiʻi’s Asset-Limited, Income-Constrained, Employed (ALICE) families making slightly above the poverty line ineligible for assistance yet unable to make ends meet. 

A universal free school meals programs is efficient and effective
Universal free school meals (UFSM) augments the traditional income-based eligibility system and removes known barriers to participation including economic, administrative, language, and social challenges. UFSM is a proven strategy to reduce childhood food insecurity and improve the overall well-being and academic performance of children. A 2022 study of schools implementing UFSM found an almost 5% decline in households classified as food insecure.[3]

Thank you for the opportunity to provide testimony in support of H.B. 1775. As shown in the attached one pager, eight states have chosen to make this worthwhile investment in their keiki. Across the country, UFSM has been implemented successfully through the intentional blending of policies and funding mechanisms at the federal, state, and local levels. While our cost estimate differs from the Department of Education we are working collaboratively to understand each other’s methodology and determine the true cost of a UFSM program. We are pleased that the Hawaiʻi legislature is paying attention to this important issue because hungry keiki cannot learn. Thank you for your consideration of this bill.
Universal Free School Meals
School Meals for All: Cost Estimates for the State of Hawaiʻi
UFSM programs offer up to two free, nutritious meals to all students in a qualifying school, regardless of their ability to pay, and are designed to improve access to and increase participation in school meals programs, especially for students with lower incomes. UFSM augments the traditional income-based eligibility system and removes known barriers to participation including economic, administrative, language, and social challenges. Program funding mechanisms vary from state to state, with many states utilizing General Fund appropriations or voter-approved tax measures on the wealthy specifically to fund their programs. The Hawaiʻi legislature is currently considering the following bills that would fund a UFSM program: H.B.1775 and S.B.2327
