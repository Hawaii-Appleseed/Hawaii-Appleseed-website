Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Comments for HB 2296 SD 1 – Relating to School Meals
Senate Committee on Ways & Means
Monday, April 6, 2026 at 10:31AM
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Dela Cruz, Vice Chair Moriwaki, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit comments for HB 2296 SD 1, which would reduce the minimum revenue requirement that the Hawaiʻi Department of Education (HIDOE) must recover through school meal charges in department schools.

We are so grateful for the legislature’s passage of SB1300 (now Act 139) last year, which extends free school meals to children under 300% of the federal poverty level. However, this goal is still hampered by language in Section 302A-405(a), Hawaiʻi Revised Statutes, which currently requires that the HIDOE charge parents at least half the cost required to produce meals. 
This version of the bill would amend that language, lowering the threshold to no less than one-quarter of the cost. However, that language still does not provide HIDOE the flexibility to extend free meals to any student going forward. Given the rising and unpredictable costs of HIDOE’s school lunch program, we believe a much fairer way to set pricing requirements would be as a cap, where parents are charged no more than one quarter of the cost. 
With this in mind, we would like to respectfully propose amending the bill back to the H.D.1 revision, which allows the Department to charge no more than one quarter of the cost of production. This clears the way for the possibility of universal free school meals by the 2029-2030 school year, aligning with Governor Josh Green’s stated commitment to achieving free school meals for all students in the next five years.[1]
School meals are one of the government’s most powerful tools for delivering good nutrition to children and supporting families. But they provide more than just nutritional benefits; they are consistently linked with better educational and health outcomes.[2]  If we want to give Hawaiʻi’s students the best chance at success, we should consider school meals just as essential as any other school supply. 
With recent numbers showing three in ten Hawaiʻi households with children face food insecurity,[3] expanding access to free school meals to all students makes sense. We urge the Committee to consider this proposed amendment to remove one of the barriers to make that possible.
Mahalo for your consideration.
