Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HCR 146 – Requesting the Department of Human Services to Apply for and Implement the Elderly Simplified Application Project in the State
House Committee on Human Services and Homelessness
Thursday, March 20, 2025, at 10:15am

                                                                                                                

Dear Chair San Buenaventura, Vice Chair Aquino, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of HCR 146, which requests the Department of Human Services (DHS) to apply for and implement the Elderly Simplified Application Project (ESAP) in the state.

SNAP is a critical lifeline for more than 160,000 Hawaiʻi residents, including approximately 31,000 seniors over the age of 60. Without it, many of these individuals would not have any money to pay for food at all. Kūpuna face unique challenges when it comes to food security. Many are living on fixed incomes and may have to juggle their own medical care, caring for grandchildren, and navigating public transportation within Hawaiʻi’s already high cost of living. 

Research has shown that older adults who are enrolled in SNAP see significant improvements in health outcomes, including:
* Less costly hospital stays and fewer hospital admissions,[1]
* A decrease in skipping medication doses to save money and/or delay refills,[2]
* Fewer nursing home admissions.[3] 

The Elderly Simplified Application Project, or ESAP, is a collection of federal waivers that makes enrolling seniors in SNAP easier. The waiver allows: 1) extending the recertification period to from one to three years; 2) eliminating the need for an interview to re-certify SNAP benefits; and 3) only requiring verification documents from the client if the information is questionable (gathering and submitting these documents is often one of the most burdensome parts of the SNAP application process). 

________________

There is evidence that the ESAP, in combination with other modifications, significantly improves the customer service experience and reduces the administrative burden in states that have adopted the waiver. In one particularly notable example, Alabama’s ESAP contributed to the addition of 42,000 additional SNAP-participating senior households from 2008 to 2016—a 21 percent increase.[4][5] A similar scale increase in Hawaiʻi would reach more than 6,500 seniors and bring Hawaiʻi’s senior SNAP participation rate up to nearly 60 percent.[6] 

As of 2024, 23 other states have already implemented ESAP and there are more to follow suit.[7] We hope that Hawaiʻi will be next. Thank you for the opportunity to provide testimony in support of HCR 146. We are pleased that the Hawaiʻi legislature is paying attention to improving food security.

________________

The Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice advocates for economic justice for and with Hawaiʻi’s people. We envision a Hawaiʻi that puts its people first—where everyone can meet their basic needs while living happy, healthy and creative lives.
