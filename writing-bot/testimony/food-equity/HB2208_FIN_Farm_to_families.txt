Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HB 2208 – Relating to Food Security
House Committee on Finance
Thursday, February 26, 2026, at 2:00PM
                                                                                                                


Dear Chair Todd, Vice Chair Takenouchi, and members of the Committee:


Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of HB 2208, which would support a Farm to Families program. This bill would appropriate additional funds to alleviate food shortages in the State by ensuring fresh produce is directed to families in need.


A strong emergency food system is a critical pillar of food equity and resiliency. This legislation is a small investment that would go a long way in strengthening local food supply chains, from supporting the local agricultural economy to guaranteeing that nutritious food is available to more families, to reducing farm food waste. 
Fresh, local produce is essential for improving community health, yet is often out of reach for many families struggling to afford life in Hawaiʻi. This program would help make healthy, whole foods available to more people, thereby alleviating hunger and preventing long-term health issues like diabetes, heart disease, stroke, and depression.[1] 
Farm to families not only strengthens Hawaiʻi’s food supply chain but also provides a backup, or “safety net” market for local farmers during times of economic crisis or natural disaster. When these events strike, the emergency food system becomes a lifeline both for families seeking food, and for farmers seeking markets when tourism plummets. During the recent federal shutdown, as the state worked toward alleviating the pause in SNAP benefits, federal workers without paychecks, SNAP recipients, and those struggling to put food on the table were pointed to the food banks. Once again, the food banks and distribution partners stepped in–institutions that historically have relied almost entirely on charitable contributions. 
By strengthening the partnerships between local farmers and our food banks, we are setting up a food system that is not as reliant on imports and puts back money in our local economy. Our state has set goals to double our local food production by 2030; passing this bill is one step to getting us there.[2]
Thank you for the opportunity to provide testimony in support of HB 2208. We are pleased that the Hawaiʻi legislature is paying attention to improving food security. By investing in this initiative, we can create a stronger, healthier, and more equitable Hawaiʻi for generations to come.
