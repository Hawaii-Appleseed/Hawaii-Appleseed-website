Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for H.B. 2430 – Relating to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program
Senate Committee on Ways and Means
Thursday, March 28, 2024, at 9:50AM
                                                                                                                

Aloha Chair Dela Cruz, Vice Chair Moriwaki, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in strong support for H.B. 2430, which would appropriate funds and establish the Summer Electronic Benefits Transfer for Children (Summer EBT) program for the state of Hawaiʻi. 

When the school year ends, children from low income households lose access to the school meals they rely on, causing an alarming annual spike in childhood hunger. While existing summer meal programs play an important role, they reach only a fraction of the children who rely on free and reduced-price school meals during the school year. Summer EBT is a new federal program that bridges this gap, by providing cash benefits on an EBT card–similar to SNAP–for families to purchase food at retail locations. 

According to the Food Research and Action Center, Hawaiʻi is home to approximately 100,000 children who are eligible to receive Summer EBT. Eligible families would receive $177 per child for this summer–up to a total of $17.7 million in federal dollars for the entire state. This is funding that would support local food businesses and retailers, while also providing significant relief to families who struggle to keep up with grocery bills while their children are not in school.

Summer EBT benefits are entirely federally funded. However, the program requires a 50 percent match in administrative costs, which HB 2430 would provide. Without this modest administrative investment, Hawaiʻi will lose out not only on the $17.7 million in federal cash benefits, but also on the economic multiplier that EBT benefits provide. In fact, the USDA estimates that every dollar in EBT benefits can generate $1.50 in local economic impact. This means that Summer EBT could generate over $26 million in economic activity during the summer months. 

HB 2430 is a small investment that will yield big results. It achieves this by ensuring Hawaiʻi has the necessary capacity to administer this important federal program, which would provide cash to struggling families during the summer months, and also by bringing in millions of federal dollars that will stimulate Hawaiʻi’s food economy. We highly encourage you to pass this bill, for the good of Hawaiʻi’s children, families, and local businesses. Thank you for your consideration.
