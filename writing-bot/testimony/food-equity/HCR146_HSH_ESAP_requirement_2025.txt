Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HCR 146 & HR 140 – Requesting the Department of Human Services to Apply for and Implement the Elderly Simplified Application Project in the State
House Committee on Human Services and Homelessness
Thursday, March 20, 2025, at 10:15am
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Marten, Vice Chair Olds, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of HCR 146 & HR 140, which requests the Department of Human Services (DHS) to apply for and implement the Elderly Simplified Application Project (ESAP) in the state.

SNAP is a critical lifeline for more than 160,000 Hawaiʻi residents, including approximately 31,000 seniors over the age of 60. Without it, many of these individuals would not have any money to pay for food at all. Research has shown that older adults who are enrolled in SNAP actually see improvements in health outcomes, including:
* Less costly hospital stays and fewer hospital admissions,[1]
* A decrease in skipping medication doses to save money and/or delay refills,[2]
* Fewer nursing home admissions.[3] 
Being enrolled in SNAP eases the burden of food costs for our kūpuna, who already have to balance medical expenses and Hawaiʻi’s high cost of living. With ESAP, our state has the opportunity to streamline the process, ensuring that those who qualify can access this essential safety net. 

The Elderly Simplified Application Project, or ESAP, is a collection of federal waivers that makes enrolling seniors in SNAP easier. The waiver allows: 1) extending the recertification period to from one to three years; 2) eliminating the need for an interview to re-certify SNAP benefits; and 3) only requiring verification documents from the client if the information is questionable (gathering and submitting these documents is often one of the most burdensome parts of the SNAP application process). 

There is evidence that the ESAP, in combination with other modifications, significantly improves the customer service experience and reduces administrative burden states that have adopted the waiver. In one particularly notable example, Alabama’s ESAP contributed to the addition of 42,000 additional SNAP-participating senior households from 2008 to 2016—a 21 percent increase.20 21 A similar scale increase in Hawaiʻi would reach more than 6,500 seniors and bring Hawaiʻi’s senior SNAP participation rate up to nearly 60 percent. 

As of 2024, 23 other states have already implemented ESAP and there are more to follow suit.[4] We hope that Hawaiʻi will be next. Thank you for the opportunity to provide testimony in support of HCR 146 & HR 140. We are pleased that the Hawaiʻi legislature is paying attention to improving food security.
