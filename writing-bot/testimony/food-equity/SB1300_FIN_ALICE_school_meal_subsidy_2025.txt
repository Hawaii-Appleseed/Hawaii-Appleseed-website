Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for S.B. 1300 – Relating to School Meals
House Committee on Finance
Tuesday, April 1, 2025, at 2:00 PM
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Yamashita, Vice Chair Takenouchi, and members of the Committee:
Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of  S.B. 1300, with suggested amendments. This bill would appropriate funds to the Hawaiʻi Department of Education to cover meal subsidies for students in households under 300 percent of the federal poverty level by the 2026- 2027 school year.
School meals are one of the government’s most powerful tools for delivering good nutrition to children and supporting families. In Hawaiʻi, three in ten households with children face food insecurity.[1] While there is a provision for some children to receive free meals, the threshold for eligibility is set at 130 percent of the federal poverty level–so a family of four would need to make under $47,000 per year to qualify.[2] This leaves thousands of families still struggling to cover their food costs,[3] and results in many children missing meals due to negative account balances. For some Hawaiʻi children, school meals are the only balanced, nutritious meals they receive in a day. 
While we wholeheartedly support any measure that would expand access to free school meals, this bill could be significantly strengthened by adding a provision requiring the DOE to expand free meals to all students by school year 2027-2028. Income thresholds are not the only barrier to participation in the free or reduced-price lunch program. Challenges like language barriers, difficulty providing paperwork, and fear of disclosing information result in many children who are eligible simply falling through the cracks. One study found that 34 percent of students who were denied free or reduced price meals were actually eligible to receive them.[4] 
SB1300 is an excellent step in the right direction, as it gets us much closer to the ultimate goal of expanding meals to all public school students. The cost estimates for these two proposals are as follows:

That said, we would like to respectfully suggest that the legislature amend the bill to:
1. Add an additional clause under HRS 302A-404 that reads: 
“Beginning with the 2027-2028 school year, free school meals shall be provided to every student enrolled in a department school.”
2. Amend HRS 302A-405, Section 2(a) to read:
“The price for school meals shall be set by the department to ensure that moneys received from the sale of the meals shall be [not less] no more than [one-half] one-quarter of the cost of preparing the meals.which requires parents to pay at least half the costs of meals.”
Across the country, Universal Free School Meal programs have been implemented successfully through the intentional blending of policies and funding mechanisms at the federal, state, and local levels. We are pleased that the Hawaiʻi legislature is paying attention to this important issue, and thank you for your consideration of this bill.
