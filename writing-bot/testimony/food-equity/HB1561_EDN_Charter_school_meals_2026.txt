Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HB 1561 – Relating to School Meals
House Committee on Education
Tuesday, February 10, 2026 at 2:15PM

                                                                                                                

Dear Chair, Vice Chair, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in strong support of HB 1561, which requires public charter schools participating in the National School Lunch Program (NSLP) to provide free meals to students whose family income is no more than three hundred per cent of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). 

We are grateful to the legislature for passing legislation last session to expand the income limit for free meals to public school students under Act 139. However, we firmly believe that this expansion should apply to all Hawaiʻi public school students, regardless of school type. The proportion of students in public charter schools considered economically disadvantaged is on par with that of HIDOE schools,[1] yet these families still need to make under $47,000 per year to qualify for this benefit.[2] Furthermore, public charter schools serve the largest proportion of Native Hawaiian students in the public school system.[3] This bill would ensure these students receive equal treatment under Hawaiʻi’s laws.
It is important to note that currently, only five of the 26 public charter schools enrolled in NSLP are not participating in the Community Eligibility Provision (CEP), a federal designation that allows schools to provide free meals to all students based on a the percentage of students who are directly certified through means-testing.[4] While we wholeheartedly support extending free meals to these five non-CEP charter schools, the way this bill is currently written, it would not provide any assistance to the other 21 NSLP charter schools, many of whom are covering the additional gap cost to provide free meals out of their own funding. 
________________

For this reason, we would like to encourage the committee to consider amending HB 1561 by adding two additional clauses under HRS §302D:
(b) Going forward, this income limit expansion shall be aligned to match that of public schools, in accordance with HRS §302A-404.
(c) The State shall cover any remaining costs for Charter schools enrolled in the Community Eligibility Provision (CEP) that fall below 62.5 percent Identified Student Percentage (ISP) (or the percentage needed to achieve the full federal reimbursement rate, subject to the multiplier required by 7 CFR 245.9(f)(4)(vi)).

The first clause intends to ensure that public charter schools will receive the same extended eligibility as other HIDOE schools going forward; the second clause addresses the gap faced by schools enrolled in CEP, and avoids the potential unintended consequence of disincentivizing enrollment. 
In case it is of interest, we have put together some cost estimates based on possible scenarios of this bill. The cost estimates for these two proposals are as follows:

	Estimated cost
	HB1561 (non-CEP schools only - CURRENT BILL VERSION)
	$206,526
	HB1561 w/ suggested amendments (assumes $1 to CEP schools)
	$616,026
	HB1561 w/ suggested amendments (assumes $1.65 to CEP schools)
	$882,202
	

Thank you for considering our testimony.

Support for BILL NO. – Relating to TOPIC
Senate/House Committee on Committee
Weekday, Month Day, 2026, at TIME
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair, Vice Chair, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of BILL NO., which requires public charter schools participating in the National School Lunch Program to provide free meals to students whose family income is no more than three hundred per cent of the federal poverty level. 

The income limit expansion to 300% FPL under Act 139 should apply to all Hawaii students, regardless of school type, providing equal access to nutritious school meals. In public charter schools, the threshold for eligibility is still set at 130 percent of the federal poverty level—so a family of four would need to make under $47,000 per year to qualify.[5] This change would eliminate an unfair gap between public schools based on SB1300 which was past last year.
School meals are one of the government’s most powerful tools for delivering good nutrition to children and supporting families. In Hawaiʻi, 34 percent of children experience food insecurity, up from 29% in 2023.[6] For some Hawaiʻi children, school meals are the only balanced, nutritious meals they receive in a day. Charter schools have a high percentage of students considered economically disadvantaged (47%), which is even 1% higher than HIDOE schools. 
Public charter schools serve Native Hawaiian students. Thirty-seven percent of students in Hawaiʻi’s public charter schools are Native Hawaiian, which is approximately 14 percent more than in HIDOE schools.[7] Excluding charter schools from expanded free meal eligibility disproportionately affects Native Hawaiian students and communities.
Charter schools play a vital role in preserving Hawaiian culture and ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi. Many public charter schools are culturally grounded learning environments, including six Hawaiian language immersion charter schools. [8] According to the Office of Hawaiian Affairs, 77 percent of the more than 4,000 students attending Hawaiian-focused charter schools are of Native Hawaiian ancestry.[9] Ensuring equitable access to school meals supports not only student well-being, but also the sustainability of these culturally and linguistically important schools.

While we support the intent of including charter schools in the expanded income limit to 300% FPL, this bill could be significantly strengthened by adding a provision that appropriates funds to charter schools participating in the Community Eligibility Provision (CEP). 
The way this bill is currently written, five public charter schools would need additional funding to ensure that they can cover the cost to produce meals while eliminating the parent fees for those who fall under the 300% FPL income limit. The estimated costs for the two proposals are below:
