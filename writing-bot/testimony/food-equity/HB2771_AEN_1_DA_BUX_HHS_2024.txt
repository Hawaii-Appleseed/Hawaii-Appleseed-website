Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for H.B. 2771 – Relating to Food Security
Senate Committees on Agriculture & Environment and Health & Human Services
Wednesday March 13, 2024, at 1:02PM
                                                                                                                

Dear Chairs Gabbard and San Buenaventura, Vice Chairs Richards and Aquino, and members of the Committees:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of H.B. 2771 which would appropriate funds for the continued administration of the Hawaiʻi Healthy Food Incentive Program and to provide matching funds to beneficiaries who participate in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP).

Hawaiʻi Appleseed is committed to a more socially and economically just Hawaiʻi, where everyone has genuine opportunities to achieve economic security and fulfill their potential. The strengthening of SNAP through incentive programs is a critical evidence-based approach to achieving this vision. Serving more than 130,000 residents and generating $630 million annually in our state’s local economy, SNAP is an economic lifeline for Hawaiʻi’s struggling households.  

2023 brought several devastating cuts to SNAP benefits in Hawaiʻi: 
1. The end of the SNAP emergency allotments left SNAP participants facing the largest SNAP benefit cliff in history. The average household lost $82 in monthly benefits and some of our most vulnerable households like those with kupuna lost up to $238.[1] 
2. The United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) updated its estimates of how much it costs the average family to buy food in each U.S. state and territory. This data is used to set the maximum amount of SNAP EBT benefits a household can receive for groceries each month. While every other state in the nation saw an increase in their monthly SNAP benefits, Hawaiʻi received a 20 percent drop in its adjustment (spread over the next five years) because of gaps in Hawaiʻi-specific food price data available to the USDA. While local advocates and the federal delegation are working hard to inform the federal government on how to remedy this issue, the benefits cut will stay in place until federal regulations are able to be changed. 

Already food insecure households are seeing hundreds of dollars less in benefits while Hawaiʻi residents continue to experience the highest cost of groceries in the nation. 

SNAP incentives like those proposed in H.B. 2771 help increase SNAP participants' purchasing power by providing an additional subsidy for SNAP users when they purchase local fruits and vegetables. The federal program that funds this incentive requires a one-to-one match, meaning that every dollar the state invests will be matched by federal funding. Therefore, a $2 million investment by the state would be doubled with matching federal funds. Factoring in the $2.10 multiplier would ultimately result in more than $8M in local economic impact.[2] Passing this bill would be an impact investment for the State, channeling much-needed capital into local businesses and farms.

Hawaiʻi’s existing SNAP incentive program, known as DA BUX, has already been enormously successful and popular among SNAP users, retailers, and farmers alike. According to the latest program outcomes report, DA BUX has already directly supported 470 farm producers, eight distributors, and increased local produce sales by 531% at farm-direct retailers.

This small investment is a win-win-win for Hawaiʻi’s families, farmers, and local economy. Thank you for the opportunity to provide testimony in support of H.B. 2771. 

Mahalo,

Jordan Smith
Senior Policy Analyst 
Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice

Aloha chairs,  vice chairs, and members of the committees

Jordan smith on behalf of Hawaii Appleseed center for law and economic justice in  support. You’ll hear only great reasons to support this legislation today even in light of the tight budget year, so I just want to emphasize the lifeline that the legislature would be extending to the more than 130,000 SNAP participants statewide by passing this measure. 

You’ll see in our written testimony that 2023 brought several devastating cuts to Hawaii's SNAP program resulting in current SNAP households receiving hundreds of dollars less in monthly benefits despite facing the highest cost of groceries in the nation.

As other testifiers mentioned DA BUX is an evidence-based program that extends SNAP participants' purchasing power for healthy foods, while increasing sales for local producers and generating millions of dollars in the local economy. 

The $2M requested investment by the state will have an outsized impact on food access and security for some of Hawaii's most vulnerable populations during this critical time. Thank you for the opportunity to testify and for your support of this measure.
