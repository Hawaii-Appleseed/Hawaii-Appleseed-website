Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for S.B. 960 – Relating to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program
House Committee on Finance
Thursday, March 27, 2025, at 3:30PM
                                                                                                                

Dear Chair Yamashita, Vice Chair Takenouchi, and members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of S.B. 960, which would appropriate funds to the Department of Human Services (DHS) to improve Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) rates, including increasing positions and pay.

Serving more than 160,000 residents, SNAP is one of the most critical public benefit programs in the nation.[1] It is an economic lifeline for these families, yet many who are eligible still struggle to obtain or maintain benefits due to bottlenecks at the state level. Despite being a critical resource for so many people, SNAP is notoriously difficult for states to administer, and for people who need it to get through the application process. Even with planned eligibility system upgrades, the program still requires “merit-based” eligibility workers to review applications, conduct interviews, and verify documents. 

DHS is working overtime to fill vacancies, streamline procedures, and alleviate bottlenecks. Eligibility and clerical workers are doing their best despite being overwhelmed and underpaid, but the situation may worsen before it improves. Because of the recent rule change to eliminate net income limits for eligibility, there is expected to be an extra 14,000 households eligible for SNAP benefits later this year.[2] With large increases in SNAP applications upcoming, it is more urgent than ever to fund this first step towards administrative efficiency. 

The SNAP office put in budget requests for performance-based incentives for eligibility workers and contracting clerical staff, but both did not make it into this year’s budget. By implementing higher pay for staff, there is more incentive to fill current vacancies and lower turnover rates, ultimately increasing internal capacity. 

Thank you for considering these comments in your deliberation of S.B. 960. The high volume of applicants are all struggling Hawaiʻi residents who need a system that works for them, not against them, and these administrative fixes are a step in the right direction.
