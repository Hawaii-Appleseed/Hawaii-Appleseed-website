Dear Chair Yamashita, Vice Chair Takenouchi, and Members of the Committee,
Thank you for the opportunity to testify on behalf of Hawaiʻi Appleseed in strong support of taxing the income generated by Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) in Hawaiʻi. This measure would close a significant loophole in the state’s tax code, ensuring that Hawaiʻi captures critical revenue from entities profiting extensively from local real estate investments.
Background
Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) are entities that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate. REITs are required to distribute at least 90% of their profits to shareholders, who then pay taxes on the income in their state of residence. While this system works for states where the majority of REIT shareholders reside, Hawaiʻi is uniquely disadvantaged because most REIT shareholders live outside the state.
Currently, Hawaiʻi exempts REIT income from state corporate taxes, allowing billions of dollars in profits generated from Hawaiʻi real estate to flow untaxed to out-of-state investors. For example, in 2014 alone, REITs in Hawaiʻi reported more than $900 million in income, yet Hawaiʻi collected no taxes on this amount. This creates a system where REITs benefit from Hawaiʻi’s real estate market and infrastructure without contributing their fair share to the state’s tax base.
Taxing REITs: A Step Toward Fairness and Equity
Implementing a tax on REIT income would help level the playing field for Hawaiʻi’s taxpayers and generate significant revenue for the state. Key benefits of this policy include:
1. Closing a Tax Loophole: Hawaiʻi is one of the few states that exempts REIT income from taxation. By eliminating this exemption, the state would ensure that REITs contribute their fair share, just like other businesses operating in Hawaiʻi.
2. Generating Critical Revenue: Taxing REIT income could generate tens of millions of dollars annually, providing funding for essential public services such as affordable housing, education, and infrastructure. These investments are vital to addressing Hawaiʻi’s housing crisis and improving the quality of life for its residents.
3. Supporting Local Businesses: Local businesses and residents bear the brunt of Hawaiʻi’s tax burden while REITs enjoy significant tax advantages. Taxing REIT income would promote tax equity, ensuring that all entities benefiting from Hawaiʻi’s economy contribute fairly to the state.
4. Encouraging Responsible Investment: Taxing REITs would not discourage investment in Hawaiʻi; rather, it would incentivize responsible and community-focused development by ensuring that profits from Hawaiʻi real estate contribute to the state’s well-being.
Conclusion
Taxing REIT income is a common-sense reform that would close a glaring loophole in Hawaiʻi’s tax code, promote fairness, and generate critical revenue to address the state’s most pressing challenges.
We urge the committee to support this measure and take an important step toward creating a more equitable and sustainable tax system for Hawaiʻi.
Mahalo for your time and consideration.
