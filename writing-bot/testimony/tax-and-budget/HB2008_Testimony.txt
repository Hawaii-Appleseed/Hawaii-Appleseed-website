Testimony of Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law & Economic Justice
Before the House Committee on Finance
 In Support of HB 2008 – High-Income Earner Tax Bracket
Dear Chair, Vice Chair, and Members of the Committee,
Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in strong support of HB 2008. Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law & Economic Justice works to build a more fair and equitable economy, and we believe HB 2008 is a well‑designed step toward ensuring that our highest-income residents contribute more in line with their ability to pay.
Why HB 2008 is needed
HB 2008 correctly identifies that income inequality in Hawaiʻi has grown sharply over the past several decades. According to findings cited in the bill, since the 1970s, the incomes of the richest households have grown roughly twice as fast as those of middle‑class households, and almost five times as fast as those of the poorest households. At the same time, a 2024 analysis by the Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy found that the top 20% of earners in Hawaiʻi pay a substantially smaller share of their income in state and local taxes than the bottom 80%, meaning that working families are carrying a heavier tax load than those at the top.
This upside‑down structure allows the highest earners to accumulate more wealth and power while many residents struggle to afford housing, food, and basic necessities. HB 2008 responds directly to this problem by asking more of those who have benefited the most from our economy.
What HB 2008 does
HB 2008 establishes a new top income tax bracket for very high‑income earners beginning with tax years after December 31, 2029. For example, with joint filers, this bill creates a new marginal rate of 16% on taxable income above $1,425,000, while leaving existing rates in place for income below that level. In practical terms, that means only the dollars earned above $1,425,000 will be subject to the new 16% marginal rate.

For single filers and married individuals filing separately, HB 2008 similarly adjusts the upper brackets so that only very high incomes see higher marginal rates, maintaining the same basic structure of lower rates on low‑ and middle‑income earnings.
This design ensures that: HB 2008 is tightly focused on a small number of very high‑income households. No one with moderate or even upper‑middle income would be affected by the new top rate.


Why this approach is sound policy
HB 2008 is grounded in strong economic research. The bill notes that Nobel Prize‑winning economist Joseph Stiglitz has described increasing top marginal income tax rates as one of the most obvious reforms to raise needed revenue and reduce harmful inequality. The bill also cites the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, which finds that higher personal income tax rates on the highest earners can generate substantial revenue for public investments and do so without harming short‑term economic growth.
Those revenues can support:
* Essential public goods such as education, health care, and infrastructure.

* Programs that help families climb the economic ladder, like housing supports, small business initiatives, and workforce development.

By funding these investments, a high‑income earner bracket like the one in HB 2008 can strengthen the broader economy and expand opportunity for residents at all income levels.
Advancing tax fairness in Hawaiʻi
HB 2008 also addresses a core fairness issue: currently, working families pay a higher share of their income in state and local taxes than the wealthiest residents. By establishing a higher marginal rate only on very high levels of income, the bill:
   * Moves Hawaiʻi’s tax system in a more progressive direction.

   * Reduces the extent to which low‑ and middle‑income households subsidize the tax advantages of the very rich.

   * Helps ensure that those who have gained the most from Hawaiʻi’s economy help maintain the public systems and services we all rely on.

This is exactly the kind of targeted, evidence‑based reform that can both raise needed revenue and reduce inequality.
Our recommendation
Hawaiʻi Appleseed strongly supports HB 2008 and urges the Committee to pass this measure. Establishing a new top bracket for high‑income earners will:
      * Ask more of those most able to pay.

      * Generate important revenue for long‑term investments in people and communities.

      * Make Hawaiʻi’s tax system fairer and more aligned with our shared values of equity and opportunity.

Mahalo for your consideration and for your work to create a more just and sustainable tax system for Hawaiʻi.
