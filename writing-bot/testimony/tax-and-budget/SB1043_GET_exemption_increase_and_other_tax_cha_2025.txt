Dear Chair, Vice Chair, and Members of the Committee,

Thank you for the opportunity to testify on behalf of Hawaiʻi Appleseed in providing comments on Senate Bill 1043, which would exempt groceries and nonprescription drugs from the General Excise Tax.

We support the fundamental idea of exempting groceries from the GET, since the cost burden tends to fall on low-income households much more than high-income households. However, it is important to acknowledge the revenue impact of doing so. Losing a substantial amount of GET revenue—in the range of $258 million to $321 million—would likely require the legislature to make budget cuts or raise taxes. Considering that Hawaiʻi is facing millions of dollars in federal spending cuts in the coming years, the state has a pressing obligation to maximize its tax revenue wherever possible. We suggest pairing any reductions in the GET with increases to progressive taxes, such as the Capital Gains Tax.

In addition, as noted by the Department of Taxation in their testimony, SB 1043’s definition of “nonprescription drugs” is open-ended, and it could include nonbulk chemicals that are not actually used to treat illnesses. To avoid unintentional consequences, we recommend amending the language of this bill by specifying the nonprescription drugs that would be excluded from the GET. For example, SB 1043 could limit this definition to nonprescription ibuprofen and acetaminophen, along with anti-histamines and antacids.

Mahalo for your consideration.
