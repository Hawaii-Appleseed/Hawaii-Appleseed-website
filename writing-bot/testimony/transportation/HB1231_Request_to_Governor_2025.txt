RE: Request to Sign Safe Routes to School Program Funding Legislation to Ensure a Safe Future for Our Keiki

Dear Governor Green,
My name is Abbey Seitz, the Director of Transportation Equity with the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law & Economic Justice. We are a non-profit organization that works towards creating a better Hawaiʻi by addressing systemic issues like housing, food security, wages, and transportation through research and advocacy, while centering the voices of those most affected. 
Hawaiʻi Appleseed respectfully requests your signature of: 
* HB 1231 HD1 SD1 CD1
* SB 1195 SD1 HD2 CD1
* SB 1009 SD2 HD2 CD1
Each of these measures provide critical funding to the Safe Routes to Schools (SRTS) program, which is dedicated to ensuring keiki can walk, bike, and roll to school by supporting pedestrian and bicycle infrastructure projects within a one-mile radius of public schools. This program has the potential to bring widespread positive change, as approximately 77% of Hawaiʻi residents live within one mile of a school.
There is an urgent need to improve the safety of vulnerable roadway users in Hawaiʻi. In 2024 alone, there were 102 traffic fatalities statewide and a staggering 42% of those fatalities involved pedestrians and bicyclists.[1] Between 2015 and 2020, nearly 49% of pedestrian fatalities on Oʻahu occurred on state-owned roadways.[2] Pedestrian fatalities and serious injuries are also growing. For example, the average annual number of statewide pedestrian fatalities rose by 26 percent during the 2014–2018 time period, when compared to the 2009–2013 time period.[3]
The SRTS Committee recently released their final report to the legislature highlighting that SRTS programs can increase walking and biking to school. More specifically, SRTS programs can increase school participation by 18-37%, while simultaneously reducing pedestrian injuries in school zones by 44-75%.[4]  Despite the importance of the SRTS program, it is severely underfunded. The current dedicated annual revenue source for the SRTS program are traffic violation surcharges, which is approximately only $500,000 per year. At the same time, there is an estimated $800 million in safe routes to school infrastructure needs across the state. A sustainable funding source, such as those in each of these bills, is critical for projects that protect our communities and keiki.

Respectfully,

Abbey Seitz
Director of Transportation Equity
