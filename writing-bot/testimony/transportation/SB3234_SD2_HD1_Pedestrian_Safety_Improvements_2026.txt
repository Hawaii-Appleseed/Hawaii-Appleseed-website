Testimony for Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice  
Support for SB3234 SD2 HD1 - Relating to Traffic Safety
House Committee on Finance (FIN)
Tuesday, April 7, 2026 at 2:00PM
________________

Dear Chair Todd, Vice Chair Takenouchi, and members of the FIN committee, 
Mahalo for the opportunity to express SUPPORT for SB3234 SD2 HD1, which would appropriate funds to the Hawaiʻi Department of Transportation (HDOT) to  to implement pedestrian safety improvements—including all-way crosswalks, raised intersections, or a combination of both—at locations abutting school properties, through allocation of monies from the state highway fund, general fund, and safe routes to school (SRTS) special fund. We also offer suggested amendments for the committee’s consideration. 

Signalized intersections are among the most dangerous places for people walking in Hawaiʻi, frequently placing pedestrians in direct conflict with turning vehicles. According to HDOT’s Vulnerable Road User Safety Assessment, more than half of all pedestrian crashes occur at intersections.[1] With roughly 20 percent of Hawaiʻi residents under the age of 18, many of our keiki rely on walking and other active modes of transportation for their independence and access to education. 

All-way crosswalks, also known as exclusive pedestrian phases, scramble crossings or Barnes Dance, utilize a traffic signal phase that stops all vehicular traffic simultaneously. This creates an exclusive window for pedestrians to cross the entire intersection at once, including diagonally, completely removing the risk of conflict with turning vehicles. Implementation of all-way crosswalks:
* Increases pedestrian visibility.
* Reduces conflicts between vehicles and pedestrians.
* Reduces pedestrian crossing time and exposure.
* Reduces the buffer zone between vehicles and pedestrians.

Research has shown significant safety improvements following the implementation of all-way crosswalks. A San Francisco study found vehicle-pedestrian conflicts decreased from 7.0 percent to 1.1 percent[2] and a Los Angeles intersection that averaged 13 crashes per year experienced zero crashes after installation of an all-way crosswalk.[3] 

It is important to note that all-way crosswalks are not a new or experimental concept to Hawaiʻi. In 2020, the City and County Department of Transportation Services successfully designed and installed an all-way crosswalk at the intersection of Kalakaua Avenue and Lewers Street in Waikiki.[4] Expanding this proven safety measure to school zones is a logical and necessary step toward achieving our Vision Zero goals.

SUGGESTED AMENDMENTS
To strengthen the bill and ensure sustainable, strategic investment in pedestrian safety, we respectfully urge the committee to remove the Safe Routes to School (SRTS) Special Fund as a Funding Source as an eligible funding source for these improvements. While we strongly support investments in pedestrian safety near schools, using the SRTS Special Fund for this purpose would be duplicative and could unintentionally undermine existing programs. The SRTS program is specifically designed to fund safety improvements within a one-mile radius of schools, and already includes an established process for project selection, including review and approval by the SRTS Advisory Committee.

Additionally, the SRTS program operates with limited funding. Even with full implementation of Act 212 (passed in 2025), the SRTS fund is expected to generate only approximately $5 to $6 million annually. In contrast, over the past decade, the state’s transportation capital budget has averaged around $700 million per year, and in 2023 alone, the State Highway Fund had a revenue of approximately $316 million.[5]

Diverting scarce SRTS funds to support this separate appropriation would reduce the State’s ability to implement other critical, community-identified safety projects through the established SRTS pipeline. Maintaining the integrity of the SRTS fund while relying on the State Highway Fund and General Fund for this initiative would better align resources with program intent and maximize overall impact.

Mahalo for the opportunity to testify on this important measure.
