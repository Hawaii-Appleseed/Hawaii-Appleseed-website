## Mission frame

Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law & Economic Justice fights for an economy where everyone in Hawaiʻi can meet basic needs — shelter, food, healthcare, mobility — afford to stay in their community, and contribute fairly. We use research, advocacy, and litigation to advance economic justice for low- and moderate-income families.

## Topic positions

### Labor & wages

**Paid family & medical leave** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for establishing a state paid family and medical leave insurance program.
- **Active bill:** HB2360 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** No working family should have to choose between a paycheck and caring for ʻohana. 90% public approval. California's 20+ year program shows mothers retain higher incomes and reduce SNAP reliance.
- **Standard ask:** Pass the bill as drafted; preserve the social-insurance model with shared employee+employer contributions.

**Pay transparency & wage equity** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for closing the small-employer carve-out in pay transparency law.
- **Active bill:** HB2367 (2026) — support eliminating the <50-employee exemption.
- **Core argument:** Hawaiʻi women earn 82.4–87% of men, *backsliding* from 92.8% in 2014. With equal pay, overall poverty drops 5.4%→2.5%; single-mother poverty 21.3%→10.7%.
- **Standard ask:** Apply pay transparency to all employers; transparency is the precondition for accountability.

**Health coverage continuity** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for state bridge coverage as federal Medicaid is cut.
- **Active bill:** HB1546 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** Federal Medicaid cuts cause "churn" — eligible people lose coverage to paperwork, not ineligibility. State must bridge for undocumented, seasonal, and independent workers. UH survey: 19% of residents carry medical debt ≥$500; 20% delay care.
- **Standard ask:** Establish bridge coverage; tie eligibility to circumstance, not status.

### Tax fairness & budget

**Capital gains tax equalization** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Support raising the capital gains rate; ultimate goal is equalization with ordinary income (11%).
- **Active bill:** HB1850 (2026) — support raise to 9% as a step toward equalization.
- **Core argument:** Hawaiʻi taxes capital gains at 7.25% vs. ordinary income at 11% — a loophole that benefits the wealthy. 70% of Hawaiʻi capital gains are earned by filers with >$400k income; the rate primarily affects those above $783k. Generates ~$55M.
- **Standard ask:** Raise to at least 9% this session; equalize with ordinary income next.

**REIT income tax** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Support actually taxing REITs — not another study.
- **Active bill:** SB2362 (2026) — support, but **amend from study to tax**.
- **Core argument:** Hawaiʻi exempts REIT income despite REITs holding $17B+ in Hawaiʻi assets. In 2014 REITs reported $900M in Hawaiʻi income and paid $0 in state corporate income tax.
- **Standard ask:** Strike the study language and impose the tax this session.

**Millionaire's income surcharge** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for a 2% surcharge on income above $1M.
- **Active bill:** HB2010 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** Generates ~$71M annually (Medicaid funding). Top 1% pays an additional ~0.72% of income. Massachusetts adopted a similar surcharge and saw continued wealth growth post-tax — the "wealth flight" argument is empirically wrong.
- **Standard ask:** Pass without watering down the threshold or the rate.

**Freeze and adjust Act 46** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for freezing Act 46 (2024 income tax cuts) and adjusting top brackets up.
- **Active bills:** HB2306 / SB3125 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** Act 46 cuts are unevenly distributed and cost the state billions. Freezing the rollout and adjusting the top 5 brackets up by 0.25–1 percentage points generates ~$69M, 86% of which comes from the top 1%.
- **Standard ask:** Freeze and adjust; treat this as a measured fiscal correction, not an attack.

**Medical debt forgiveness** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for state-funded medical debt acquisition and forgiveness.
- **Active bill:** SB3025 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** Use the proven non-profit acquisition model (27 states/cities now do this) to forgive medical debt for households ≤400% FPL or with debt ≥5% of income. Medical debt cascades into delayed care, housing instability, and worse health.
- **Standard ask:** Fund the program at the level needed to retire material debt for eligible households.

**Conveyance tax modernization** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for marginal-rate conveyance tax with progressive rate structure and dedicated revenue.
- **Active bill:** HB2049 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** The conveyance tax hasn't been updated since 2009. Modernize to marginal rates; protect <$2.2M owner-occupied homes; raise rates on high-value investment properties; index to inflation. Dedicate revenue: affordable housing (20%), DHHL (30%), DURF (20%), conservation (5%). Hawaiʻi needs 50,000+ housing units; DHHL waitlist is 6,000 families ($800M backlog).
- **Standard ask:** Pass HB2049 as drafted; resist amendments that strip the dedicated funding allocations.
- **Signature framing:** "those who profit most from our islands also invest most in their future"; "housing ahead of handcuffs" (the $300M prison budget vs. $185M DHHL contrast).

### Housing & renters

**Renter protections** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Comprehensive renter protections package.
- **Asks (from "Renters Left Behind," 2025):**
  - Just-cause eviction
  - Right to legal counsel for tenants in eviction proceedings
  - 60-day notice for lease non-renewal and rent increases
  - Real enforcement mechanisms (not voluntary compliance)
- **Core argument:** 38% of Hawaiʻi households rent. Maui and Kauaʻi have among the highest median rents in the country. Renters are routinely sidelined in housing policy despite carrying the highest cost burden.
- **Recent wins to cite:** Act 57 (2021) mandatory mediation became a national model; Act 237 (2022) raised TANF by $500.

**Active 2026 bills:** HB1732, HB1776, HB1777 — `[REVIEW positions]` (sample testimony exists in `testimony/housing/` but should be confirmed against final bill text).

**Housing tax credits** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Support strengthening WCTC, federal/state EITC, and a state-level child tax credit.
- **Core argument:** 15,664 residents left Hawaiʻi in 2022 due to cost of living. Refundable tax credits put money in working families' hands for everyday expenses — food, rent, medical care.

### Food equity

**Universal free school meals** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for universal free school meals.
- **Active bills:** HB1779 (2026) — support universal by 2030; HB2296 (2026) — support removing the revenue-requirement obstacle.
- **Core argument:** "School meals are essential school supplies." 3 in 10 households with children face food insecurity. The current means-tested system fails: a family of 4 earning $47k+ doesn't qualify, even though ~$107k is needed to cover living costs in Hawaiʻi. 34% of denied students are actually eligible — they fall through the paperwork. For some keiki, school meals are the only balanced meals they receive.
- **Standard ask:** Pass HB1779; pass HB2296; reject phase-ins that delay implementation past 2030.
- **Charter school equity note:** Hawaiʻi charter schools serve 14% more Native Hawaiian students than DOE schools and many incorporate local/canoe crops — universal coverage matters for cultural reasons too.

**SNAP access** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for extending SNAP certification and adopting the federal ESAP waiver.
- **Active bill:** SB3245 (2026) — support.
- **Core argument:** 160,000+ Hawaiʻi residents depend on SNAP. Extending certification from 12→24 months and adopting ESAP (up to 36 months for seniors) reduces "churn" — eligible people losing benefits to paperwork. 23 states already use ESAP.
- **Standard ask:** Pass without rollback; align with federal options.

### Transportation

**Transit equity** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Support fare-free transit for keiki, state employees, and pursuit of universal fare-free.
- **Active bills:** HB1879 (Keiki Ride Free, $9.87M/year), SB2993 (state employees), HB2451 (all users) — support.
- **Core argument:** Removing cost barriers expands youth and disability access. Locally proven: Maui <24 fare-free, Hawaiʻi County fully fare-free. Nationally proven: Washington state free <18, Kansas City fare-free saw ridership surge.
- **Budget framing:** 66% of HDOT's $5.68B (FY19–24) went to vehicle capacity; only 1.5% to transit alternatives. Keiki Ride Free is 0.87% of the 5-year budget — proportional to the need.
- **Climate connection:** Tied to *Navahine v. HDOT* and the 2045 decarbonization mandate.

**Pedestrian safety** `[REVIEW]`
- **Position:** Strong support for Leading Pedestrian Intervals (LPIs) and Accessible Pedestrian Signals (APS).
- **Active bills:** HB1884 / SB2470 (2026, "Pedestrian Head Start") — support.
- **Core argument:** A 3–7 second head start at intersections measurably saves lives. Seattle: 48% reduction in turning collisions, 34% reduction in serious/fatal pedestrian injuries. NYC 2025 results: 33% injury reduction, 65% fatal reduction. Installation cost: hundreds to a few thousand dollars per intersection. Disproportionately benefits keiki, kūpuna, people with disabilities, and those relying on walking and transit.
- **Standard ask:** Pass with funding for HDOT to deploy at high-risk intersections statewide.

## Voice signature

### Testimony format
- **Open:** "Dear Chair [Name], Vice Chair [Name], and Members of the Committee, / Thank you for the opportunity to testify in [strong support of / opposition to / comments on] [bill number] — Relating to [subject]."
- **Body:** State position → urgent context with named populations → evidence (specific stats, dollar amounts, comparable jurisdictions) → counter the predictable opposition → restate the ask.
- **Close:** "Mahalo for the opportunity to testify" + the specific action requested + signature.
- **Signature:** "Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice"

### Blog post arc
Problem → why the system fails the people most affected → data and named harms → proven model elsewhere → the bill in front of us. Often opens with a values-grounded sentence about Hawaiʻi as place and people, not policy.

### Language conventions

**Always use:**
- ʻOkina on **Hawaiʻi**, **ʻohana**, **ʻāina**, **kūpuna** (and other Hawaiian words where appropriate)
- Kahakō where standard (kūpuna, ʻāina, ʻohana, mānoa, etc.)
- "Working families" — not "low-income families" or "the poor"
- Specific named populations — keiki, kūpuna, women, single mothers, undocumented workers, disabled residents, Native Hawaiians — over abstract "vulnerable populations"

**Recurring values vocabulary:** dignity, ʻohana, keiki, kūpuna, fair share, essential, barrier, lifeline, contribute, stabilize, thrive, opportunity, just, equity.

**Recurring frames:**
- "Fair share" / "those who profit most also invest most"
- "Close the loophole"
- "Proven model" / "already working in [N] states"
- "Barriers" — paperwork, cost, fear, stigma — emphasized over individual fault
- "Essential" — moral frame for what working families need
- Cascading harm — wage gap → poverty → child poverty; medical debt → delayed care → housing instability

**Avoid:**
- Bureaucratic distance ("constituents," "stakeholders," "demographics")
- Hedging that obscures the ask ("we encourage consideration of") — be direct
- Technocratic detachment when describing harm — name who is hurt
- Overuse of Hawaiian words for decoration; use them where they carry meaning

### Evidence style
Heavy on specific numbers, dollar amounts, named jurisdictions, and bill numbers. Always cite the source. Explain technical concepts accessibly (e.g., marginal rates illustrated with a dollar example, not just a definition). Pair the moral case with the practical case — "unfair *and* it works because…"

### Tone
Urgent but measured. We are advocates, not screamers. Confident in the position, generous to good-faith opposition, unsparing about consequences. We speak as Hawaiʻi, to Hawaiʻi.
