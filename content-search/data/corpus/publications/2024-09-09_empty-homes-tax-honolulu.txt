2 • EMPTY HOMES TAX

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MORE THAN SIX IN 10 RENTERS IN HONOLULU SPEND AT LEAST 

30 PERCENT OF THEIR INCOME ON HOUSING COSTS.

THE CITY & COUNTY OF HONOLULU, covering the 
entire island of Oʻahu, is facing a severe housing 
crisis—one that has grown worse, in part, because 
wealthy investors purchase homes as investment 
properties. By doing this, these investors leave homes 
empty instead of renting them out for people to live in. 

Across Honolulu, an estimated 35,000 units are 
currently sitting empty.1 Many of them have been 
vacant for long periods of time, and this restriction of 
available units has driven up prices and made it harder 
for local residents to find affordable rentals.

Rental prices in Honolulu have long been inflated. The 
median rent for a one-bedroom apartment in Honolulu 
in 2024 is $2,100, compared to $1,600 on average in 
the  entire U.S.2 More than six in 10 renters in Honolulu 
spend at least 30 percent of their income on housing 
costs.3 This makes them “severely housing cost-
burdened,” meaning they have limited money to pay 
for their other needs, like food and transportation.4

The Honolulu City Council is considering Bill 46 (2024), 
which would impose an Empty Homes Tax (EHT) of 3 
percent on properties that are vacant for more than 6 
months each year (both non-owner-occupied and non-
renter-occupied). If homes are occupied for 6 months, 
they would also have to be occupied in periods of at 
least 3 consecutive months. 

Currently, investment-owners who choose to turn their 
properties into vacation rentals—or keep them vacant 
for most of the year—do not have to pay an extra tax, 

even though they are driving up the value of real estate 
and taking units off the rental market. 

Honolulu already uses part of its property tax revenues 
to support long-term affordable housing. An EHT 
could provide the county with additional funding for 
the development of more affordable housing for local 
residents while discouraging investors from using 
Oʻahu’s housing supply as an investment alone. 

This brief helps inform the discussion around Bill 
46 by presenting EHT best practices based on case 
studies of other cities with high-cost housing markets 
that have implemented their own EHTs. Based on this 
analysis, Hawaiʻi Appleseed recommends a flat tax of 
3–5 percent on empty homes. This tax could generate 
revenues of $183.40 million–$305.67 million annually.5 

A well-crafted policy would also: 
•  Require homeowners to make an annual

EMPTY HOMES TAX • 3

HOW DOES AN EMPTY 
HOMES TAX WORK?

The Empty Homes Tax (EHT) is based 
on the idea that owners of unoccupied 
homes should pay higher tax rates on 
their vacant properties. 

In addition, the EHT targets properties 
that only wealthier buyers can 
afford. For example, in Vancouver, 
British Colombia, empty homes are 
often worth much more than the 
average property. In 2022, vacant 
condominiums were worth 60 percent 
more than the average condominium, 
and vacant single-family homes were 
worth 48 percent more than the 
average single-family home.6

Empty home taxes are increasingly 
being implemented in various cities 
around the world. The case studies 
of Vancouver, British Columbia, in 
Canada, Washington, D.C., and Maui 
County, Hawaiʻi provide valuable 
insights into the successes and failures 
of different EHTs. 

Generally, an empty homes tax seeks to achieve the following objectives:

• 

Supporting Housing Initiatives: Revenue from these taxes is often directed towards affordable housing and 
homelessness services, and addressing broader social issues related to housing.

•  Reducing Speculative Investment: A primary goal across all these cities is to make more housing available 

by discouraging the practice of using vacant homes as an investment.7

By reducing speculative investment and generating new revenue to support housing initiatives, an EHT seeks 
to rebalance a city’s housing market, so that it works better for local residents, rather than wealthy outside 
investors and speculators.

KEY FINDINGS

Revenue Generation and 
Conversions

The implementation of the EHT 
in Vancouver has proven to be a 
significant revenue generator, with 
a total of $154 million collected 
since the program began in 
2017. This revenue generation 
has been accompanied by a 
54 percent reduction in vacant 
properties between 2017 and 
2022, demonstrating the program’s 
effectiveness in encouraging property 
owners to either sell or convert their 
properties into long-term rentals.17 

Data on D.C.’s revenue from the tax is 
incomplete. However, in combining 
data in 2016, 2019 and 2022, 
Appleseed found D.C. generated $99 
million in tax revenue.18 An increase 
in vacant properties was noted 
through the years, and is likely due 
to better enforcement. However, this 
cannot be confirmed due to the lack 
of a baseline for previous occupancy 
status from D.C.’s registration method. 

Maui’s LTR program, while well-
intentioned, has resulted in over 
$17.7 million in lost revenue over two 
years.19 20 The lost revenue from the 
LTR subsidy has a direct impact on 
Maui County’s budget, of which 40.4 
percent is made up of Real Property 
Tax revenue.21 The tax subsidy has 
reduced revenues at a time when the 
county needs funds for both increased 
housing initiatives and other essential 
services, raising concerns about its 
overall effectiveness.

EMPTY HOMES TAX • 7

KEY FINDINGS

Declaration of Occupied or Vacant Status

In Washington, D.C., property owners only need to register once their property becomes vacant. This reliance 
on self-reporting can lead to unregistered and unmonitored vacancies. The compliance rate in D.C. was just 33 
percent in 2017.22

Vancouver’s model, by contrast, allows for better oversight because it requires all property owners to declare 
their property’s status annually—either vacant or occupied. This system sets a baseline of each property that 
allows the city to determine conversion rates and allows for better compliance measuring and enforcement, 
resulting in a sterling compliance rate of 99.3 percent in 2022.23

Maui County requires proof of occupancy to qualify for its LTR subsidy. There is no concern over unregistered 
properties since those properties wouldn’t receive a subsidy without a declaration.

Occupancy Requirement

The goal of the EHT is to ensure housing stock is used to house residents. It is not intended to punish homeowners 
with valid reasons for keeping their property vacant. It is important to recognize that exemptions will have to exist. 
Yet, at the same time, exemptions increase the complexity and workload of administering the EHT. 

To mitigate this problem, the occupancy requirement should be structured so that it captures the intended 
units—mainly vacant properties that exist purely for speculative purposes, and short-term rentals. At the same 
time, the county should not be requiring an exemption filing for every reasonable occurrence. 

Vancouver’s residency requirement for the EHT also allows for nonconsecutive occupancy, provided that the 
principal residence is occupied for periods of 30 or more consecutive days.24 This approach effectively exempts 
month-to-month rentals from the EHT, while still targeting the majority of short-term rentals.

EHTs commonly provide exemptions for primary residences, properties under construction or renovation, and 
property owners who are in the hospital or long-term care. The exemptions for an EHT should be clear-cut and 
focused on helping homeowners who use their property as a primary residence or a long-term rental.

Penalties 

In Washington, D.C., property owners face a $2,000 fine for failing to register their property, and another $5,000 
for failing to register after notice. Additionally, submitting a false registration can result in a $1,000 fine, as well 
as back pay of taxes.25 Washington, D.C. experienced high non-compliance, likely because avoiding the tax is 
more beneficial than risking the fines associated with it.

In Vancouver, the EHT imposes a 3 percent tax on the property’s assessed value if it remains undeclared after 
the declaration deadline. This is accompanied by an additional penalty of 5 percent of the empty homes tax on 
the property. False declarations of property status can lead to fines of up to $10,000 per day, in addition to the 
required tax payment.26

Maui’s LTR program is likely far too lenient. The only penalty is the back payment of property tax owed and a civil 
penalty maximum of $200 if falsely declared.27

8 • EMPTY HOMES TAX

KEY FINDINGS

Administration and Technical Assistance Needs

Vancouver secures administration costs for its program by first allocating revenue from their EHT to cover annual 
operating costs, estimated at $2.5 million, before directing the remaining funds to affordable housing initiatives.28

Washington, D.C. does not have any portion of its EHT specifically allocated for administration of the tax. It was 
initially financed through the city budget’s general fund, and additional funds have had to be added throughout 
the years to maintain enforcement and improve compliance.29 

The Real Property Assessment Division manages the LTR program on Maui, but there is no available data on the 
administrative needs for its implementation.

Revenue Use

After allocation of EHT revenue into annual and upfront administration costs, Vancouver has poured the rest of 
its revenue into developing affordable housing. A majority of the funding goes into Vancouver’s CHIP program, 
as well as city land acquisition. The CHIP program provides grants to nonprofit housing organizations to support 
the construction, operation and maintenance of long-term affordable housing, with the city providing the land. 

Since the development of CHIP and the implementation of the EHT to fund it, Vancouver has approved 1,221 
social and supportive housing units—on track with their annual targets.30

EMPTY HOMES TAX • 9

RECOMMENDATIONS

Given the global demand for Honolulu real estate, and an increasing trend of homes being left vacant for 
speculative purposes, the City & County of Honolulu should implement an Empty Homes Tax that will discourage 
speculative investment, while also raising millions in revenue for much needed city services. 

Having reviewed EHT models both here in Hawaiʻi and abroad, Hawaiʻi Appleseed recommends the City & County 
Honolulu create an EHT that includes the following components:

1. Flat Tax of 3–5%

The proposal to levy a 3 percent tax on empty homes is modeled on Vancouver’s successful EHT, which supports 
the city’s development of affordable housing on city land. If Honolulu were to implement a 3 percent tax on 
empty homes, it could generate $183.4 million in new revenue for county services each year. A more ambitious 
tax of 5 percent could generate $305.67 million annually.

While a graduated tax is better than a flat tax, because it takes into account the differences in property value and 
usage, it may also require a higher administrative burden to implement. Additionally, none of the case studies 
reviewed used graduated tax rates, making a flat tax the more defined option due to its ease of implementation 
and proof of concept.

2. Annual Declaration of Occupancy Status 

Any EHT should require a mandatory, annual declaration of property status as either occupied or vacant. The 
City & County of Honolulu should develop an online portal for ease of both declaration and audit, along with the 
option for community reporting of vacant properties similar to Honolulu’s community investigation request for 
short-term rentals.32

3. 6-Month Residency Requirement (In Periods of 30 or More Consecutive Days)

The EHT should be targeted at residential properties that are not the primary residence of a homeowner, renter 
or other permitted occupant who is also an Oʻahu resident for at least 6 months of the tax year, and that do not 
meet any EHT exemptions. 

The EHT aims to either bring homes that are not typically on the rental market back into active use, or subject 
them to taxation. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, common reasons for vacancies include properties listed 
for sale, available for rent, or those in the process of foreclosure or eviction.33 While the length of these situations 
can vary, implementing a 6-month occupancy requirement allows the county to exempt homes that are actively 
on the market, but are temporarily experiencing vacancy. A residency requirement of 6 months, in periods of 30 
or more consecutive days, would still capture 33 percent of Honolulu’s vacant homes.34 *35

Allowing for nonconsecutive occupancy offers valuable flexibility for tenants in Honolulu in the form of month 
to month leases. This flexibility is particularly important for individuals in transition, such as those between

RECOMMENDATIONS

jobs, relocating for work, or experiencing life changes like divorce or retirement. It also helps fill a critical gap for 
visiting professionals, students, and those waiting for their new homes to be ready.

4. Limited Exemptions

Honolulu’s EHT should exempt the following from the tax to ensure it is targeted to second home owners and 
speculative buyers:
•  New units until they are sold by the developer;
•  Homeowners with valid medical reasons for not occupying their home;
•  Homeowners affected by natural disasters;
•  Homeowners with properties under construction or renovation; and
•  Homeowners who are renting their unit to long-term tenants.

5. Penalties 

Honolulu should impose a late declaration fee, as well as a false declaration fee charged for each day the 
property is non-compliant. Fees must be high enough to discourage homeowners from skirting the EHT.

6. Administration and Technical Assistance

A portion of the EHT revenue should be directed to the Honolulu Department of Budget & Fiscal Services (BFS) to 
ensure sufficient annual funding for staff needs and database-building. Investing in a comprehensive database 
with robust enforcement mechanisms and sufficient staff is crucial for the success of an EHT tax in Honolulu. 

BFS would likely be the best department to oversee the administration of this tax. This department already 
handles property taxes and other financial matters for the county. The administration of the EHT would logically 
fit within its existing responsibilities.  

There is already an existing need for the City & County of Honolulu to develop a more effective database on 
property use. In 2022, Honolulu passed a strict tax on short-term rentals, with a registration system overseen by 
the Honolulu Department of Planning & Permitting. 

7. Revenue Use

All revenue, outside of administration costs, should be allocated toward housing initiatives. This can be achieved 
by either creating a special fund with earmarked revenue that allows for broader housing initiatives, or by 
directing the revenue into the Affordable Housing Fund while working to loosen its current restrictions. Given 
the severe housing crisis in Honolulu, Hawaiʻi Appleseed strongly recommends that all revenue from the EHT be 
dedicated exclusively to funding a wide range of affordable housing initiatives. 

Honolulu needs a flexible approach to respond to the county’s evolving housing challenges. A rigid allocation 
could limit the county government’s ability to address diverse needs related to housing, such as rental 
assistance, down payment assistance, infrastructure development, or renovation of existing units. Flexibility 
allows the county to adapt to changes in the housing market and prioritize projects based on urgency, 
maximizing the fund’s potential. Currently, the only county-level fund dedicated to affordable housing in 
Honolulu is the Affordable Housing Fund, however the fund’s use is tightly restricted.36

REASONS FOR VACANT 
PROPERTIES

Speculative Investment

Many homebuyers who come to Honolulu from 
elsewhere are looking for second homes—13 percent 
of all housing units in the City & County of Honolulu 
are owned by people who do not live in Hawaiʻi.37 
Some of these buyers do not purchase homes to live 
in or to rent out but, instead, to sell later at a profit. 
This practice, which is called speculative investment, 
removes homes from the housing market for sale and 
rental, contributing to the higher prices that make 
Honolulu unaffordable for working class families.38 

In 2023, 10.1 percent of single-family homes and 
17.3 percent of condominiums in Honolulu were sold 
to out-of-state buyers.39 On Oʻahu, prices of units 
purchased by out-of-state buyers in 2018 were 46.6 
percent higher than the average units sold to locals.40 

When people from outside buy in Hawaiʻi, they often 
pay higher prices. This sets a new standard for the 
market value of homes, which then raises the prices 
of other homes in the same area. Since these prices 
tend to rise faster than the average income in Hawaiʻi, 
it becomes more challenging for local residents to 
afford homes. This continues to price the average 
local resident out of the housing market.

In addition, Hawaiʻi has a unique appeal as a tourist 
destination and luxury real estate market. Data 
shows that home prices in Hawaiʻi have increased 
400 percent since 2000.41 For investment-owners, it 
is obvious that there are lucrative returns possible 
from holding onto real estate in high-demand areas 
like Honolulu. These investments capitalize on the 
long-term appreciation of property values without the 
need for rental income.

Short Term Rentals (STRs)

Oʻahu is a top global tourist destination, attracting 5.6 million visitors in 2023 alone.42 This creates a strong demand 
for short-term vacation rentals. In June of 2024, there were 872,620 visitors to Hawaiʻi. Around 24.8 percent of these 
visitors stayed in either condominiums or rental houses, compared to the 58.3 percent who stayed in hotels.43 Due 
to Oʻahu’s low property tax rates, investors can easily profit from this demand by buying properties and renting 
them out to tourists. Since the beginning of 2021, Oʻahu has seen an 18 percent increase in STRs.44

According to AirDNA, a short term rental data analytics site, the average rate for a vacation rental this past year in 
Honolulu was $360.60 per night. The average monthly revenue for short term rental owners was $6,550/month, 
significantly higher than the current market rental rate of $1,800 per month.45 On average, short-term rentals in 
Hawaiʻi earn almost four times more than if the properties were leased long-term to local families. Because of this, 
it is more profitable for investors to use their home as a vacation rental instead of a long-term rental.

Wealthy real estate buyers take advantage of Honolulu’s roads, highways, culture and natural beauty, all of which 
have made it a desirable place to live. However, they pay little in property taxes compared to many other high-cost 
cities in the U.S., such as New York City or San Francisco. Honolulu’s property tax rate in comparison to New York 
City is significantly lower, ranging from 0.35 percent to 1.05 percent, while the rate for New York City can be as high 
as 20 percent.

Vacant homes do not contribute to the local economy in the same way occupied homes do. They do not 
support local businesses, schools, or other community resources. This makes them a drain on public resources, 
contributing to a decline in services and quality of life.48 While, Constitutionally, the government  cannot ban 
investors from buying homes, the City & County of Honolulu can tax them at a higher rate to offset some of the 
harm vacant homes cause to our housing market by funneling additional tax revenue to social services and
