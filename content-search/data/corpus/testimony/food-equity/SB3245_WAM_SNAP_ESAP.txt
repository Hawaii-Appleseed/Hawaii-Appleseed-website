Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for SB 3245 – Relating to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program
Senate Committee on Ways and Means
Wednesday, February 25, 2026, at 10:56AM
                                                                                                                


Dear Chair Dela Cruz, Vice Chair Moriwaki, and members of the Committee:


Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of SB 3245, which would require the Department of Human Services (DHS) to adjust the minimum certification periods for Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits households, participate in the Elderly Simplified Application Project (ESAP), and report to the Legislature.


SNAP is a critical lifeline for more than 160,000 Hawaiʻi residents. Without it, many of these individuals would not have any money to pay for food at all. Since benefits are federally funded, federal rules require tedious paperwork and documentation both upon initial application and for periodic recertification.[1] Some SNAP users lose these crucial benefits during the recertification process, despite no changes to eligibility. This phenomenon, known as “churn,” adds significantly to the administrative burden of administering SNAP at the state level.


This bill would provide some relief for the churn issue. Currently, Hawaiʻi requires recertification every 12 months for most households (with a six-month “interim report”). However, federal rules allow states to extend the SNAP certification period for certain households to 24 months. Using the ESAP waiver, the recertification period can be even longer–up to 36 months, and the waiver removes some additional application barriers for seniors. As of 2024, 23 other states have already implemented ESAP and there are more to follow suit.[2] 


In fact, the Legislature has already requested that DHS implement this waiver, through last year’s adoption of HCR146 HD1 SD1 (2025), which requested “DHS to apply for and implement the Elderly Simplified Application Project in the State when capable” and to “seek any necessary federal approvals or waivers to implement the ESAP in the State, including amending its rules, policies, and procedures.” This bill formalizes this request into a mandate, and addresses the “when capable” clause by setting the effective date to July 1, 2028. 


The proposed extended certification periods for most households have twofold benefits, reducing workload for both applicants and eligibility staff. We have a unique opportunity to ease the burden on both sides of the application process. Thank you for considering these comments in your deliberation of SB 3245.
