Testimony of the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for SB 2211 – Relating to Making an Emergency Appropriation to the Department of Human Services.
House Committee on Human Services and Homelessness
Thursday, March 19, 2026, 9:30AM
                                                                                                                


Dear Chair Marten, Vice Chair Olds, and members of the Committee:


Thank you for the opportunity to submit testimony in support of SB 2211, which would make an emergency appropriation to the Department of Human Services (DHS) to provide emergency assistance to households participating in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) and to expand food delivery services through its contracted community-based providers, including to supplement or expand the Hawaiʻi Emergency Food Assistance Program (HEFAP).


SNAP is a critical lifeline for more than 155,000 Hawaiʻi residents, supporting working families, individuals with disabilities, and others living on fixed or limited incomes.[1] Without it, many of these individuals would not have any money to pay for food at all. Additionally, 1 in 3 Hawaiʻi residents remain food insecure, struggling with the state’s continued high cost of living.[2]


During the federal shutdown, Hawaiʻi acted rapidly in distributing an emergency benefit of $250 in food assistance, by leveraging the existing Electronic Benefits Transfer (EBT) system.[3] HEFAP and community partners helped households bridge the gap created by the disruption in federal SNAP funding—an approach this bill would strengthen and build upon. Moreover, expanding food delivery services through contracted providers can help reach populations that may otherwise face barriers to accessing nutritious food, including kūpuna, people with disabilities, and those living in remote areas. 


SNAP is the largest and most critical public benefit program in the state, and its federal funding is projected to be reduced under H.R. 1. These changes will directly constrain the level of food assistance available to Hawaiʻi households, increasing the risk of benefit reductions or gaps in assistance in the coming years. SB 2211 provides Hawaiʻi with a necessary safeguard, ensuring the state has the capacity to respond quickly and protect residents from the downstream impacts of federal funding cuts.


We appreciate the Legislature’s consideration of SB 2211 as an important measure to strengthen Hawaiʻi’s food safety net during times of uncertainty. By ensuring that emergency resources can be deployed quickly and equitably, this bill helps protect households who are most vulnerable to disruptions in food access.
