Aloha Chair, Vice Chair, and members of the committee,
I am testifying in strong support of HB2451, the Free Transit for All bill. This legislation represents the single most transformative step we can take toward a sustainable, equitable, and functional transportation system in Hawaiʻi. By requiring counties to provide fare-free transit access to all residents and funding it through a modest increase in the barrel tax, this bill addresses three interconnected crises at once: climate, affordability, and mobility.

On Climate: Transportation remains our state's largest source of greenhouse gas emissions. Decades of incremental encouragement have not moved the needle fast enough. Free transit removes the single greatest barrier to mode shift—cost—and makes riding the bus the logical, default choice for thousands of daily trips. More riders means fewer cars, less congestion, and a direct path toward our 2045 zero-emissions goal. This is climate action at scale.
On Affordability: Hawaiʻi families are drowning in the highest cost of living in the nation. For a working parent commuting to multiple jobs, for a student navigating school and activities, for a kupuna on fixed income—transit fares are a monthly expense that adds up fast. Eliminating fares puts real money back into pockets and ensures that no one is stranded because they cannot afford a bus ride.
On Equity and Mobility: Free transit for all is not a subsidy for the few; it is public infrastructure functioning as public infrastructure should. We do not charge residents a fee to use public roads, sidewalks, or libraries. Transit is no different. It is the circulatory system of our communities, and universal access strengthens the whole. Students get to school. Workers get to jobs. Families get to appointments. Visitors get to experiences. Everyone moves more freely.
The funding mechanism—an increase in the barrel tax—is both appropriate and just. The barrel tax is paid by those who import and consume fossil fuels, the very source of the pollution free transit is designed to reduce. It aligns the cost with the problem, ensuring that the transition to clean mobility is paid for by the industry driving the crisis, not by working families.
HB2451 HD1 is bold, necessary, and achievable. It invests in our people, our climate, and our shared future. I urge you to pass this bill and make free transit for all a reality in Hawaiʻi.
Mahalo for the opportunity to testify.
