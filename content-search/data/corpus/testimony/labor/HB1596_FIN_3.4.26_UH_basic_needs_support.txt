Testimony of the Hawai‘i Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
Support for HB 1596 – Relating to Student Basic Needs Support
House Committee on Finance
Wednesday, March 4, 2026, at 10:00 AM
                                                                                                                


Dear Chair Todd, Vice Chair Takenouchi, and members of the Committee,
Thank you for the opportunity to testify in strong support of HB1596.
Hawaiʻi Appleseed supports policies that ensure low-income and working-class residents of Hawaiʻi can meet their basic needs and thrive. Access to higher education is one of the most powerful pathways to economic mobility—but students cannot succeed academically if they are struggling to afford rent, food, transportation, or healthcare.
HB1596 would establish a limited basic needs pilot program at one campus within the University of Hawaiʻi system, including a dedicated benefits navigator and the option to implement a housing stability pilot project. This is a thoughtful, fiscally responsible approach: start small, gather Hawaiʻi-specific data, and evaluate outcomes before considering broader expansion.
A campus-based benefits navigator can help students access public benefits, emergency assistance, and community resources that already exist but are often difficult to navigate. By improving coordination and awareness, the State can better leverage existing federal and state dollars to stabilize students and support their persistence.
The bill’s required annual survey and legislative reporting will provide critical data on housing stability, food security, service use, and academic outcomes. Given Hawaiʻi’s high cost of living, local data is essential to guide sound policy decisions.
Even modest, targeted support—particularly for students experiencing housing instability—can make the difference between dropping out and completing a degree. That is a smart investment in both individual opportunity and our state’s workforce and economic future.
Thank you for considering this important bill.
