Dear Chair, Vice Chair, and Members of the Committee,
Thank you for the opportunity to testify in strong support of establishing a wealth proceeds tax in Hawaiʻi. We believe this policy is a fair and targeted way to raise substantial revenue from the very wealthiest residents, while protecting low- and middle-income families and helping to address our state’s deep inequities.
Why Hawaiʻi needs a wealth proceeds tax
Hawaiʻi has one of the highest costs of living in the nation, and many families are struggling to keep up with housing, food, and other basic needs. At the same time, a small number of ultra‑wealthy households hold a disproportionate share of assets and investment gains. A wealth proceeds tax would ensure that a portion of these very large asset gains is taxed so that those at the top contribute more in line with their ability to pay.​
This approach shifts part of the tax burden away from workers and consumers and toward large concentrations of wealth, allowing the state to fund essential services without raising broad-based taxes.​
How the wealth proceeds tax works
Under the proposal, Hawaiʻi would levy a tax on very large “wealth proceeds” — significant gains from assets such as stocks, closely held businesses, or other high‑value investments — once those gains exceed a high threshold.​
Key design elements include:
* The tax applies only to extremely large one‑time or occasional gains, well above the levels typical for ordinary homeowners or small investors.​
* Retirement accounts and modest asset sales would not be affected because the threshold is set high enough that only the ultra‑rich would pay.​
Because of this design, the tax would fall overwhelmingly on the top fraction of 1% of Hawaiʻi residents, leaving the vast majority of households untouched.​
Revenue and equity impact
A wealth proceeds tax structured in this way is projected to raise hundreds of millions of dollars over time, with the annual amount depending on market conditions and the number of very large asset sales in a given year.​
* The vast majority of revenue would come from a relatively small group of ultra‑wealthy taxpayers whose large asset gains are currently taxed lightly compared to wage income.​
* This helps reduce the upside‑down nature of Hawaiʻi’s tax system, in which low‑ and middle‑income households currently pay a higher share of their income in state and local taxes than the richest residents.​
By directing this new revenue toward housing, education, health care, and climate resilience, Hawaiʻi can make meaningful progress on its most pressing challenges while narrowing wealth and racial inequities.​
Our request
We respectfully urge the Committee to pass the wealth proceeds tax and to dedicate the resulting revenue to priorities that directly benefit Hawaiʻi’s working families and vulnerable communities. This reform would take a modest, reasonable step toward asking the ultra‑rich to contribute more fairly, while helping to build a more stable and just future for our state.​
Mahalo for your time and consideration.
