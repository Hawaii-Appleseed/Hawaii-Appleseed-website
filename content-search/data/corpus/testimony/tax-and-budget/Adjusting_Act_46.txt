Dear Chair, Vice Chair, and Members of the Committee,
Thank you for the opportunity to testify in strong support of freezing and adjusting Act 46 (2024). Hawaiʻi Appleseed advocates for a fair, balanced tax system that supports essential public services while reducing inequality. Freezing the current rollout of Act 46 and adjusting its top income tax brackets would help Hawaiʻi maintain fiscal stability and ensure that our tax code works for everyone, not just the wealthiest residents.
Why this action is needed
Act 46’s income tax cuts are unevenly distributed and will cost the state billions of dollars over the next several years. While all households see some tax relief, the largest benefits go to the highest-income earners — those least in need of additional support. With federal funding uncertain and state costs rising, Hawaiʻi cannot afford deep revenue losses that threaten vital programs such as education, health care, and housing.
Freezing the rollout of Act 46 provides the Legislature time to reassess its fiscal impact and ensure the state’s long-term financial health.
Making Act 46 more fair
To move toward a more balanced solution, we support freezing the income tax brackets at their current levels in 2026 and modestly adjusting the top five income tax rates. These changes would bring in an estimated $69 million in additional revenue while keeping the system largely intact for most taxpayers. The proposed changes would adjust the rates as follows:

According to fiscal analysis, 86% of the total $69 million revenue gain would come from the top 1% of earners (those making over $783,000 annually), ensuring that middle- and low-income residents are not burdened.
The path forward
Freezing and adjusting Act 46 is a balanced, fair approach that restores critical revenue while maintaining relief where it’s most needed. This step will help protect public services, strengthen economic resilience, and ensure that Hawaiʻi’s tax system advances equity instead of deepening inequality.
We respectfully urge the Committee to support this measure and keep Hawaiʻi on a sustainable fiscal path that invests in its people and communities.
Mahalo for the opportunity to testify.
Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice
