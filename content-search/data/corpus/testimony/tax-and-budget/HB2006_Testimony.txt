Dear Chair Marten, Vice Chair Olds, and Members of the Committee
Mahalo for the opportunity to submit testimony in strong support of HB 2006. 
Many of Hawaiʻi’s families are struggling to get by, especially if they have young children. In fact, more than 41% of our population is in the ALICE category—meaning even though they are employed, they cannot afford all of their basic necessities. In considering the future of Hawaiʻi’s keiki, we must look at the substantial research proving that direct, unconditional cash support during the prenatal and early infant stages is the most effective way to help families in need.
Proven success of RxKids
The model for HB 2006 is based on the groundbreaking Rx Kids program in Flint, Michigan. Early data from 2024 reveals that this approach does not just provide a safety net—it boosts outcomes for children and their families in the long term:
* Maternal Mental Health: Program participants saw a 14-percentage-point reduction in screenings for postpartum depression and a significant decrease in generalized anxiety.
* Housing & Food Security: Research showed a 4.2-percentage-point reduction in the risk of eviction and a 12.5-percentage-point decline in families reporting they did not have enough food.
* Improved Birth Outcomes: The program is associated with increases in prenatal care visits and significant reductions in preterm births and low birth weights, which are primary drivers of infant mortality.
* Financial stability in the home results in healthy brain development.
* Increased Brain Activity: in regions associated with language, memory, and executive function.
* Environmental Stability: Reduced parental "cognitive load" and stress, allowing for more high-quality interaction between parent and child.
All of these points are in line with evidence from other assistance programs like the Child Tax Credit. During the 2021 expansion of the federal Child Tax Credit, the U.S. saw child poverty slashed to a historic low of 5.2%.
Investing in our future
HB 2006 is a fiscally responsible investment that utilizes Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) funds to prevent high-cost social issues later.
* The 13-to-1 Return: National economic analyses suggest that for every $1 invested in early childhood (0–3), society sees a return of up to $13.00 through increased future earnings, better educational attainment, and reduced healthcare and criminal justice costs.
* Civic Trust: Recent data indicates that mothers enrolled in Rx Kids show a 5-percentage-point increase in voter turnout and higher levels of trust in government, fostering a more engaged and stable community.


Conclusion
The data is clear: cash benefits for mothers are an effective tool to break the cycle of intergenerational poverty. HB 2006 allows Hawaiʻi to lead where the federal government has paused, ensuring our state is a place where local families can afford to stay and thrive.
I urge you to pass HB 2006. Mahalo for your commitment to our keiki.
