Wealth Proceeds Tax & Capital Gains
Policy Workshop


Attendees: Sarah Austin, Devin Thomas, Tanya Yamanaka, Michele McCoy, Jonathan Huskey, Nate Hix, Nicole Woo, Kris Coffield, Mylia Pax, Will White, Trisha Kajimura, Abby Bachofor, Christy MacPherson, Michele McCoy, Daniela Spoto, Nikos Leverenz, Kim Coco Iwamoto, Younghee Overly,


Resources:
* Devin's presentation on capital gains and blog post on capital gains
* Sarah's brief on the wealth proceeds tax (includes charts used in her presentation)
* Sarah’s slides
Quick recap
The meeting focused on discussing two tax proposals: a capital gains tax and a wealth proceeds tax, with Devin providing an overview of Hawaii's current capital gains tax system and potential rate adjustments. The group explored the legislative implications and revenue potential of a wealth proceeds tax, examining how it could address income disparity and be adapted for Hawaii's specific needs. After discussing various concerns and considerations, including potential impacts on older residents and messaging strategies, the group agreed to form a policy working group to further explore these tax proposals before the legislative session begins.
Next steps
* Will: Follow up with Rep Hussey on her status with the Wealth Proceeds Tax bill and see where she's at with sending it for drafting
* Will: Follow up with Jackson Sayama to understand his thinking on the 9% capital gains bill for this session
* Daniela: Circulate Sarah's slides and materials from the presentation to coalition members after the call
* Daniela: Circulate Sarah's report with state-by-state revenue estimates to coalition members
* Coalition: Schedule a policy working group meeting before session begins to continue discussion on both capital gains and wealth proceeds tax proposals
* Sarah: Provide model language for the Wealth Proceeds Tax that can be adapted to Hawaii law
* Coalition: Have conversation about messaging strategy for both bills and whether to put coalition weight behind both proposals
Summary
Hawaii Wealth Tax Discussion
Daniela and Sarah discussed the potential impact of a wealth proceeds tax in Hawaii, noting that while it wasn't a coalition priority, there was interest from a legislator to introduce it. They acknowledged the need to gather more data on how this tax would specifically affect Hawaii. Devin, an expert on capital gains tax, was expected to join the meeting to provide insights into the legislative status and nuances of the tax.
Hawaii Capital Gains Tax Proposals
The meeting focused on discussing two tax proposals: the capital gains tax and the wealth proceeds tax. Devin presented an overview of the capital gains tax, explaining that it currently has a preferential rate of 7.25% in Hawaii and primarily benefits the wealthy. He outlined two potential proposals: taxing capital gains at the same rate as ordinary income, which could raise $85 million, or increasing the rate to 9% to raise $44 million. The group discussed opposition to closing the capital gains loophole, with arguments centering on punishing successful investments and affecting small business investment. The conversation ended with Rep. Yamoto emphasizing the need to address these concerns with strong evidence and clear messaging, particularly given the current state budget situation.
Wealth Proceeds Tax Discussion
Sarah presented on the Wealth Proceeds Tax, explaining how it would address the disparity between tax rates for wage income and wealth income, similar to Hawaii's efforts. She detailed the tax's potential revenue for states like Hawaii, highlighting that it would primarily affect millionaires and could raise significant funds. The discussion touched on legislative strategies and the importance of securing commitments for roll call votes from senators.
Minnesota Tax Model for Hawaii
The group discussed Minnesota's wealth proceeds tax, which imposes a 1% tax on income over $1 million and brings in about $60 million annually. They explored how this tax could be adapted for Hawaii, including potential thresholds and carve-outs for real estate. Nicole raised concerns about how the tax might affect older residents selling their homes, and the group discussed ways to address this, such as allowing prepayment of capital gains taxes. Will reported that Rep. Hussey is interested in introducing a similar bill in Hawaii and is considering model language, though she has some reservations about certain provisions. The group agreed to follow up with Rep. Hussey on the status of her bill and to consider strategically messaging any potential dual tax proposals.
Tax Proposals and Policy Planning
The group discussed two tax proposals: a 9% capital gains tax and a wealth proceeds tax. Will expressed concerns about the capital gains proposal's lack of traction, while Nicole and others argued for its potential benefits and suggested aligning thresholds with the wealth proceeds tax. The group agreed to continue discussions on messaging and supporting both bills, with Younghee expressing preference for the wealth proceeds tax due to its higher revenue potential. They decided to form a policy working group to further explore these options before the legislative session begins.

Messaging Guide
A Fair Tax System to Fund Hawaiʻi’s Future


3 Topline Messages
1. Goods for the Common Good 
Taxes pay for things that help our communities thrive, like housing, healthcare, food security, roads, schools, and care for our keiki and kupuna. Our local businesses rely on these investments to produce a healthy and educated workforce they need to succeed. These investments are the building blocks of a healthy, thriving community and we deserve to have the very best.  Fair taxation is how we’ll get there.


2. Everyone Pays Their Fair Share
Tax fairness means those with more pay more. A tax system that ensures everyone pays what they can afford—especially those who have benefited most from Hawaiʻi’s land, resources, and public infrastructure - ensures a resilient local economy. Asking those with over $1M household income to pay their fair share is not only responsible–it’s smart. The ultra-wealthy will still be wealthy, even if their taxes go up a couple of percentage points. 


3. The Choice is Clear, and the Time is Now
Federal cuts to critical safety net programs, an affordability crisis, and the growing threat of climate change mean that the State could be facing an unprecedented budget shortfall that cannot simply be ignored. Our policymakers have 3 very clear choices:  
   1. Austerity: Cut education programs and public services, putting an even larger strain on already struggling families and community resources
   2. Issue bonds: This is essentially using the State’s “credit card,” passing more debt on to future generations.
   3. Apply taxes fairly to grow the middle class, support the needs of our communities, and to fund our future.

Tax Fairness / Fund Our Future - We Need Your Help!


Who We Are
The Hawaiʻi Tax Fairness Coalition is a statewide alliance of nonprofits, community organizations, unions, advocates, researchers, and service providers working to create a more equitable tax system—one that raises the revenue our state needs without placing the burden on low- and moderate-income families.
We advocate for practical, achievable reforms that ensure those who benefit most from Hawaiʻi’s economy contribute fairly so the state can sustainably fund housing, healthcare, education, infrastructure, and essential community services.
________________


A Fair Tax System: The “Why” and the “How”
A fair tax system isn’t just good economic policy—it’s foundational to a healthy democracy.
* Funds essential services. It ensures Hawaiʻi can sustain strong public services and safety-net programs, which are the backbone of stable democracies worldwide.
* Builds public trust. People lose faith in government when they see loopholes for the wealthy or when working families carry more than their share. Fair taxation shows that the rules apply evenly, strengthening confidence in our institutions.
* Balances political power. When wealth is overly concentrated, a small group can exert outsized influence over policy and elections. We’re seeing this play out now with federal cuts to essential programs like SNAP and Medicaid—decisions that don’t reflect the needs of most people.
So how do we get there? Our Core Policy Priorities
A fair tax system is one where the wealth gap shrinks after taxes are paid—not grows. But in Hawaiʻi, the opposite is happening: the wealthiest pay a smaller share of their income than those with the least, widening the gap every year. To fix this, we pursue two main strategies: 
1. Raise Revenue to Support Critical Services. In 2026, especially after recent federal cuts, our top priority is restoring sustainable state revenue by:
* Modernizing the Income Tax: Freeze the income tax cuts passed in 2024. Restore higher marginal rates for top earners.
* Closing the Capital Gains Loophole: Tax investment income the same as wages.
* Reforming Property & Real Estate Taxes: Increase conveyance tax rates on luxury and investment properties, and ensure Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) contribute appropriately.
2. Strengthen Credits for Working Families. Our coalition also supports expanding refundable credits—like the EITC, child credits, dependent care credits, and the food excise tax credit—to boost take-home income for low- and moderate-income households.

💌 Org Sign-on Letter
  



Aloha Members of the Hawaiʻi State Legislature,
We, the undersigned organizations and small businesses, join in support of the Fund Our Future legislative package to advance tax fairness and generate sustainable revenue for Hawaiʻi’s long-term needs.
As nonprofits, service providers, small businesses, and community partners, we see firsthand how high cost of living, housing instability, underfunded public systems, and widening economic inequality are straining our communities. Hawaiʻi’s local families and small businesses are struggling with rising costs and federal cuts, while the wealthiest individuals and corporations pay a smaller share of their income in taxes. 
A fair and balanced revenue system is essential to adequately fund core public priorities such as healthcare, affordable housing, food security, education, climate resilience, and care for our keiki and kūpuna. We support the Fund Our Future reforms to generate funding for these essential needs by having the ultra-wealthy pay their fair share. This includes proposals that would: 
* Stop Tax Cuts for the Wealthy – Act 46 of 2024 is a sweeping tax cut that disproportionately benefits the ultra wealthy– providing the top 1% with an average annual tax break of $12,000 while reducing state revenues by more than $1.4 billion by 2031. We support freezing income tax cuts to the high income earners and reworking these tax cuts to favor working families. Revenue Potential: $296 million+ per year.
* Tax Wealth Like Work – Hawaiʻi is one of only nine states that gives preferential tax treatment to capital gains, allowing wealthy investors to pay lower rates than what working people pay on their wages. This bill would end that special treatment. The top 5% would contribute 97% of the revenue, while 80% of Hawaiʻi households would see no increase at all. Revenue Potential: $84 million+ per year.
* Increase the Mansion Sales Tax – Also known as a conveyance tax, this is a one-time tax paid when a luxury home is sold. This proposal would raise rates on luxury home sales from 0.5% - 1.25% to 2% - 6% for homes worth $2+ million, while protecting middle-income homeowners. Revenue Potential: $68.5 million per year. 
* Close the REITs Tax Loophole – Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) make millions in real estate profits but pay no corporate income tax to our state. These policies would ensure large REITs pay their fair share for benefiting from public infrastructure and services. Revenue Potential: $36 - $60 million per year.
* Create a Millionaire’s Income Tax – U.S. Congress passed the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act” in July 2025, giving a federal tax cut of $43,000 on average to those earning $1 million or more. Adding a modest 2% surcharge on income above $1 million would support shared investment in our state’s future. Revenue Potential: $71 million+ per year.


Taken together, the Fund Our Future package could raise Hawaiʻi $584.5 million while creating a fairer system that supports our collective future — one where working families are supported, local businesses can grow, and communities are resilient. 


We urge the Legislature to advance these measures and to prioritize long-term fiscal sustainability, equity, and shared prosperity for Hawaiʻi.
Mahalo for your leadership and for the opportunity to provide this letter of support.
