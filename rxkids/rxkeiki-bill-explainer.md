# Hawaiʻi Rx Keiki: what the draft 2027 bill does

Working explainer for staff. Source: "Hawaii Rx Keiki Program (DRAFT 2027 Bill)", LRB 2027-0063, in the RxKeiki Drive folder. The draft adds a new section to HRS chapter 346 and takes effect July 1, 2027. Cost figures come from the HIPHI cost slide used on the RxKeiki page (`index.html` in this folder).

This describes a draft. Section references (346-(c), (f), (g)) follow the draft's own lettering and may change.

## 1. Universality

- **No income or asset test.** 346-(c): no one may be required to meet one.
- **No benefit-enrollment condition.** Families do not have to be on Medicaid, TANF, or any other public benefit.
- **Verification only.** The department may set "reasonable requirements" to verify three things: pregnancy, or the birth or guardianship of the child; identity; and Hawaiʻi residency.
- **No strings on the money.** Payments cannot be conditioned on employment, education, child support cooperation, health care use, or how the payment is spent.
- **Who counts.** An eligible pregnant individual is at least 16 weeks pregnant and lives in Hawaiʻi. An eligible child is under 1 year old and lives in Hawaiʻi.
- **Same payments for everyone.** DHS may adjust amounts for cost of living, but the same schedule applies to every participant in the same program period.

## 2. Payments

| Payment | Amount | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Prenatal | $1,500 once, payable from 16 weeks of gestation | Core, subject to appropriation |
| Months 1–6 after birth | $500 per month, from birth through the month the child turns 6 months | Core, subject to appropriation |
| Months 7–12 | $500 per month, through the month the child turns 12 months | "Subject to available funds" only |

The core program is $4,500 per baby. With the optional months it is $7,500. The whole program is "subject to legislative appropriation and the availability of funds."

## 3. How funding is braided

The bill uses two separate mechanisms. They solve different problems.

### 3a. Which funding stream pays (the TANF split)

TANF stays "no strings" only if it pays for nonrecurrent, short-term benefits. So 346-(f) limits TANF in two ways.

- **Which payments.** TANF may pay only for the $1,500 prenatal payment and no more than the first three monthly payments. That is $3,000 per family. HHS may approve a different qualifying period in writing.
- **Which families.** Only households that meet the means test in the state TANF plan. DHS may use Medicaid eligibility as a proxy, or set a separate income standard.
- **Everyone else.** Later payments, and every payment to a family that does not qualify for TANF, come from state, county, private, or other non-TANF funds.
- **Same payments for everyone.** Both kinds of family receive identical amounts on identical dates. DHS or the administrator sorts the funding source behind the scenes and keeps records of which dollar came from where.
- **No TANF strings.** A payment is not ongoing TANF cash assistance, so it triggers no time limit, work requirement, or child support assignment.

Example:

| Payment | Medicaid-eligible family | Higher-income family |
|---|---|---|
| Prenatal, $1,500 | TANF | State/county/private |
| Months 1–3, $500 each | TANF | State/county/private |
| Months 4–6, $500 each | State/county/private | State/county/private |

### 3b. The $1 private gift (benefits protection)

Government cash can count as income and reduce SNAP, Medicaid, and similar benefits. A charitable gift from a nonprofit is more likely to be excluded.

- **The rule (346-(g)(1)).** Any payment that includes TANF or other governmental funds must also include at least $1 from a bona fide nongovernmental, philanthropic source. A qualified nonprofit administrator issues it as one blended payment.
- **The characterization (g)(2).** DHS structures and documents each blended payment as a charitable gift from the nonprofit to the participant, to the extent federal and state law permit. This does not change how the underlying government funds are accounted for or reported.
- **$1 is a minimum.** A payment made entirely from private money does not need it. Nearly every payment will contain government money, because TANF, state, and county dollars all count as governmental. In practice almost every payment carries the $1.
- **Example.** A $500 monthly payment might be $499 of state dollars and $1 of philanthropy, delivered as a single $500 payment from the nonprofit.
- **Alternative structure.** DHS may use a different structure if it determines in writing that the structure protects benefits equally or better.

## 4. Benefit protections

- **SNAP.** DHS must use the federal option at 7 CFR 273.9(c)(19) and amend the state SNAP plan to exclude payments from income.
- **Other programs.** DHS must exclude payments from income and resources for TANF, Medicaid, and child care assistance, and seek any state plan amendment, waiver, or approval that requires. It must coordinate with other state and county agencies.
- **Notice before enrollment (g)(6).** Each applicant gets a plain-language notice describing how major programs are expected to treat the payment and naming any program with no exclusion secured. SSI recipients, and people on Medicaid through SSI, are advised to see a benefits counselor first.
- **No guarantee (h).** Nothing in the section guarantees exclusion where federal law requires otherwise.

## 5. How people apply

The bill barely describes an application. It sets guardrails and leaves the mechanics to DHS and a nonprofit administrator, to be set by rule.

**What the bill says**

- Verification is limited to pregnancy or birth or guardianship, identity, and residency.
- DHS may contract with nonprofit third-party administrators to operate the program, verify eligibility, enroll participants, issue payments, provide benefits counseling and customer service, prevent fraud, and keep records.
- DHS may contract with community-based organizations for outreach, application assistance, navigation, language access, benefits counseling, and referrals.
- DHS may adopt rules under chapter 91, including interim rules for up to 18 months after the effective date.

**What the bill does not say**

- Whether enrollment is online, by paper, by phone, or in person.
- Which documents count as proof of pregnancy, identity, or residency.
- Whether people enroll during pregnancy or after birth. The prenatal payment can start at 16 weeks, so a prenatal path is implied but not spelled out.
- How a family learns which funding stream covers it. The administrator would handle that behind the scenes.
- Appeals, deadlines, or re-enrollment steps.

Possible amendment topics if the application process should be pinned down: accepted documents, a no-wrong-door option through the community partners, presumptive prenatal enrollment, and a single combined notice.

## 6. Administration and financing

- **Cost cap.** Administration may not exceed 5% of total program expenditures. The legislature may authorize a higher percentage during the first year of statewide implementation.
- **Funding sources.** DHS may accept federal, state, county, and private funds, grants, gifts, and donations, and may combine public and private funds in a single payment. It must keep records that identify the amount and source of every dollar.
- **Procurement.** Contracts under 346-(d) are exempt from chapters 103D and 103F, as long as DHS ensures transparency, accountability, and reasonable competition.
- **Staffing.** DHS may establish one permanent program specialist and one permanent fiscal specialist.
- **Appropriations.** Section 6 appropriates TANF funds and Section 7 appropriates general funds, each for FY 2027-28 and FY 2028-29. **Both dollar amounts are blank in the draft.**

## 7. Preconditions before any payment (Section 3)

1. Submit the TANF and SNAP state plan amendments needed to implement the Act.
2. Seek written guidance or approval from HHS and USDA on the financing, nonrecurrent short-term benefit, and income-exclusion structures.
3. Execute an agreement with a qualified nonprofit administrator and confirm enough private funds to put at least $1 in every blended payment supported by government money.
4. Publish a program financing and benefits-protection implementation plan on the DHS website.

The program cannot start July 1, 2027 unless the federal approvals arrive first. That is the main timing risk.

## 8. Reporting

DHS reports to the legislature no later than 20 days before each regular session. The report covers enrollment and payment data, geographic and demographic distribution, spending by funding source, administrative costs, private funds raised, federal approvals or plan amendments, how other benefit programs treated the payments, and available well-being findings. Data is aggregate and privacy-protected.

## 9. Cost estimate

The bill has no dollar figure. These numbers are from the HIPHI cost slide: 14,808 babies (2023 births), with TANF covering $3,000 per Medicaid birth.

| Version | Total | TANF covers | Other public or private dollars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prenatal + 6 months ($4,500 per baby) | **$66.6M** | $15.3M | $51.4M |
| Prenatal + 12 months ($7,500 per baby) | $111.1M | $15.3M | $95.8M |

The 12-month total is derived: the slide's $95.8M of other dollars plus $15.3M of TANF.

**By county, six-month version**

| County | Babies | Total | TANF | Other dollars needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Honolulu | 10,474 | $47.1M | $9.1M | $38.0M |
| Hawaiʻi | 2,055 | $9.2M | $3.2M | $6.0M |
| Maui | 1,566 | $7.0M | $2.0M | $5.1M |

$15.255M of TANF at $3,000 each implies about 5,085 Medicaid births, roughly a third of all births.

**Not included in these figures:** administration (up to 5%, roughly $3.3M on the six-month version), the private match, the two DHS positions, cost-of-living adjustments, and take-up below 100%. The slide prices every birth. The slide's birth year (2023) differs from the 2022 shown on the HIPHI one-pager; confirm before citing.

## 10. Open questions in the draft

- The appropriation amounts are blank.
- Months 7–12 depend on available funds, so the base program is six months.
- The $1 gift structure and the SNAP and Medicaid exclusions depend on federal agencies agreeing. Section 8 makes any part of the Act inoperative to the extent it conflicts with federal requirements.
- The claim that this model mirrors how Rx Kids runs in Michigan has not been checked against Michigan's own documents.

## 11. Companion credits: income limits

Related to the 2027 credits priority. Source for the food/excise tables is the Census-Forecaster model (`packages/tax_modeler/.../hi_food_excise.py`).

### Refundable food/excise tax credit (HRS §235-55.85)

A flat amount per exemption (filer, spouse, each dependent), set by federal AGI.

**Taxable years 2023–2027 (Act 163)**

| AGI under | Per exemption |
|---|---|
| $15,000 | $220 |
| $20,000 | $200 |
| $25,000 | $170 |
| $30,000 | $140 |
| $40,000 | $110 |
| $50,000 (joint table only) | $90 |
| $60,000 (joint table only) | $70 |

The credit ends at $40,000 AGI on the single table and $60,000 on the joint table. The joint table covers married filing jointly, head of household, surviving spouse, and spouses filing separately.

**From TY2028**, Act 163 is repealed and the earlier table returns. The 2026 session did not extend it (HB 2306 was not enacted).

| AGI under | Per exemption |
|---|---|
| $5,000 | $110 |
| $10,000 | $100 |
| $15,000 | $85 |
| $20,000 | $70 |
| $30,000 (single cutoff) | $55 |
| $40,000 (joint only) | $45 |
| $50,000 (joint cutoff) | $35 |

The model does not apply two statutory limits: no extra exemption for age 65+ or disability, and presence in Hawaiʻi more than nine months.

### State earned income tax credit (HRS §235-55.75)

Hawaiʻi has no income table of its own. The credit is a percentage of the federal EITC, so the federal income limits decide who qualifies.

- **TY2023–2027:** 40% of the federal credit (Act 163). Refundable.
- **From TY2028:** 20% of the federal credit.

Federal EITC income limits for tax year 2025. **These are from memory of IRS figures and have not been checked against the IRS revenue procedure; verify before citing.**

| Children | Single / head of household | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| None | $19,104 | $26,214 |
| 1 | $50,434 | $57,554 |
| 2 | $57,310 | $64,430 |
| 3 or more | $61,555 | $68,675 |

The federal investment-income limit is $11,950. Federal limits are indexed, so later years are higher.
