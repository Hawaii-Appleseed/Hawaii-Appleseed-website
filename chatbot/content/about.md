## Who we are
<!-- url: /our-mission -->

Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law & Economic Justice is a Honolulu nonprofit that advances economic justice for and with the people of Hawaiʻi. Its office is at 733 Bishop Street, Suite 1180, Honolulu, Hawaiʻi 96813. The organization is part of the national Appleseed Center network.

Its vision is a more socially and economically just Hawaiʻi, where everyone has genuine opportunities to achieve economic security and fulfill their potential.

## How Hawaiʻi Appleseed works
<!-- url: /our-mission -->

Five practices shape the work:

1. Community engagement to surface real barriers and solutions.
2. Data-driven research and analysis on inequities.
3. Education for policymakers and the public.
4. Collaborative problem-solving and coalition building.
5. Advocacy for state, local, and federal systems change.

Five core values guide it: Collaboration (working with diverse partners to build durable connections), People First (change must center those most adversely impacted by current systems), Equity (naming structural inequities, including racism, colonialism, and oppression), Courage (speaking up against powerful interests for people-first policies), and Integrity (work that is accurate, reliable, and aligned with stated values).

The guiding principle is that those closest to the problem are closest to the solution. Hawaiʻi Appleseed integrates people with lived experience of these issues into its leadership and decision-making.

## Issue areas
<!-- url: /issues -->

Hawaiʻi Appleseed works on five areas:

- **Affordable Housing:** tenant protections and housing stability, rent stabilization, social housing and affordable supply, reducing zoning and parking barriers, and climate resilience and disaster recovery.
- **Food Equity:** SNAP, universal school meals, and access to culturally significant, nutritious food and a stronger local food economy.
- **Wages & Labor:** fair wages and protections that match the cost of living in Hawaiʻi.
- **Transportation Equity:** safe, accessible, affordable transportation, including fare-free transit for keiki, safer streets, and equitable traffic enforcement.
- **Taxes & Budget:** a fairer tax code, working family tax credits such as the EITC, budget transparency, and closing corporate tax loopholes.

Each area has a page on the website: /affordable-housing, /food-equity, /wages-labor, /transportation-equity, and /taxes-budget.

## History
<!-- url: /our-history -->

The organization was founded in 2004 as Lawyers for Equal Justice by a group of attorneys and advocates concerned about poverty and inequality in Hawaiʻi. Its first case challenged rent overcharges in subsidized housing and led to rent reductions of more than $1 million every year since it was resolved. Victor Geminiani led the early work, focused on housing rights, public benefits, and economic security.

In 2011 it became the Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice and joined the national Appleseed network. The shift recognized that enforcing existing laws was not enough; real change would require new laws, new budgets, and new public investment. Gavin Thornton, the organization's first employee, later served as its second executive director. Will White is the third executive director.

Early class-action and litigation work included access to public education for homeless children (2007–08), upgrades at Kuhio Park Terrace and Mayor Wright Homes (2008–15), healthcare access for Compact of Free Association (COFA) residents (2010–14), timely SNAP benefit processing (2010–11), and language translation on driver's license forms and tests (2013–15). A 2013 lawsuit over foster care payments ended in 2017 when the Legislature raised foster care rates.

## Milestones and wins
<!-- url: /our-history -->

- 2017: Hawaiʻi's first state Earned Income Tax Credit, worth $20 million in tax relief each year. It was expanded in 2022 to $40 million a year and better targeted to low-income families.
- 2018: Joined the State Priorities Partnership and founded the Hawaiʻi Budget & Policy Center, since folded into Hawaiʻi Appleseed.
- 2019: Helped establish DA BUX, a SNAP doubling program for local produce.
- 2020–2022: During COVID-19, Hawaiʻi adopted Appleseed's proposal for an emergency rent relief and eviction mediation program.
- 2022: The Legislature passed a phased increase of the minimum wage from $10.10 to $18 an hour, estimated to deliver $1 billion in additional wages a year to more than 200,000 workers.
- 2022: A $1 billion state investment in affordable housing development, which Appleseed's research and advocacy helped drive.
- 2018–2023: The Front Street apartments case in Lahaina. Appleseed sued in 2018 and won a 2020 judgment holding the developer to a 51-year affordability covenant. The apartments were destroyed in the 2023 Lahaina wildfire.
- 2026: The Legislature and the Governor agreed to partially roll back Act 46, the tax cuts that primarily benefited wealthier residents.

The organization's own summary of its record: more than 20 years of advocacy, $1 billion or more in added wages each year, $40 million in annual tax relief, and more than 200,000 workers reached.

## Community power
<!-- url: /our-history -->

A growing part of the work is helping community members advocate for themselves, through workshops, training programs, and organizing. Appleseed's biggest legislative wins have come with a critical mass of community support and participation. The Pilina Builders leadership program, for example, trains a cohort of women from ʻEwa and Waipahu.

## Ways to help
<!-- url: /support -->

- **Give:** donations fund the legal research, policy advocacy, and community education behind the work. Monthly giving is described as the most impactful way to sustain it year-round. The Donate page offers monthly gifts of $10 (the Seedling), $50 (the Advocate), and $150 (the Champion), or another amount, one time or recurring.
- **Follow the policy work:** read the research and follow the bills Appleseed is tracking each session. Each issue area has its own coalition, with action alerts for the legislation you care about most.
- **Contact your legislator:** the action center helps people find their legislators, see where they stand on tracked bills, and send a message in their own words.
- **Legacy gifts:** including Hawaiʻi Appleseed in estate planning.

## What this assistant can and cannot do
<!-- url: /our-mission -->

This assistant answers questions about Hawaiʻi Appleseed, its work, its research, and its published policy positions. It cannot give legal advice or legal help, take or discuss anyone's personal legal case, sign anyone up for anything, accept donations or personal information, or speak for other organizations. For anything it cannot answer, the office's phone and email (on the Contact page) and the Our Team page are the best next step.
